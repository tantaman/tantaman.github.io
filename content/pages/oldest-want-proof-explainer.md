# The Oldest Want: A Plain English Explanation

The proof next to this one uses formal notation — definitions, axioms, lemmas, a theorem. This is what it says.

---

## What we're proving

There is a want underneath all other wants. Not hunger, not safety, not pleasure — those have ceilings. You can have enough of them.

This want has no ceiling. It is the want to be fully known by another person. Not the version of you they can see. Not the self you presented so they could receive it. The whole thing. Including the parts that didn't make it into any version you've ever shown anyone.

We are going to prove that this want cannot, in principle, be satisfied by any other human being. Not because people are selfish or distracted or unloving. Because of the structure of attention and the nature of physical bodies.

And then we are going to say what a satisfaction would have to look like, if one exists.

---

## The basic facts we start from

Two things are true about the physical world that we take as given.

The first: bodies are finite. You are somewhere. You are not everywhere. You occupy a position, you take up space, you exist at a particular angle to everything else. This is just what it means to have a body.

The second: attention is directional. When another person pays attention to you, their attention is not a floodlight that illuminates everything equally in all directions. It is more like a beam. It lands on what enters its path. It cannot receive what lies outside its field.

These two facts, taken together, produce everything that follows.

---

## The trap

To be seen by another person, you have to be seeable by them. You have to enter their field of attention. You have to take a shape they can receive — position yourself, make yourself legible, present a form of yourself that their attention can land on.

This is not optional. It is not a failure of courage or authenticity. It is physics. Attention requires an object. An object requires a form. You cannot offer your full self as the object, because your full self is not a form — it is the thing that precedes and exceeds every form you have ever taken.

So what gets seen is the form. The shaped, positioned, receivable version of you. And what doesn't get seen — what we call the remainder — is everything that didn't make it into the form.

Here is the problem: the remainder is not incidental. It is not the boring parts, the unimportant overflow. The remainder is the part that wanted to be seen. It is the full subject, the irreducible you, the thing that has no form because it is the source of all your forms.

The move toward being seen requires a form. The form excludes the remainder. The remainder is what wanted to be seen. Therefore the move toward being seen is simultaneously a move away from the thing that wanted to be seen.

You reach. The reaching conceals what was reaching.

---

## Why better people don't solve it

A natural response: the problem is that people aren't attentive enough, generous enough, present enough. If we had better lovers, better friends, better communities — people who really looked, who were truly willing to see — the problem would dissolve.

It wouldn't.

The constraint is not the other person's willingness. It is the structure of their attention. Even a maximally generous, maximally present, maximally loving other can only receive what enters their attentional field. And entering their attentional field requires you to take a form. And any form excludes the remainder.

Generosity changes how much of your form is received. It does not change the fact that what is received is a form. The physics holds regardless of the quality of the relationship.

This is why the loneliness at the center of even the best relationships is not a sign that you chose wrong. It is a sign that you are a subject and the other person is embodied. The grief is structural, not personal.

---

## Why refusing the want doesn't solve it either

Another response: stop wanting it. The Stoic suppression of it. The Buddhist dissolution of desire. The underground man's furious refusal to need anyone to see him.

This doesn't work either, for a precise reason.

To refuse the want is to want something: to be seen as someone who does not need to be seen. And that wanting is legible. It takes a form — the form of the person who has transcended need, who is self-sufficient, who does not require recognition. That form can be presented to others. That form will be received. And the remainder — the part of you that still wants, underneath the refusal — goes unseen.

The refusal is subject to the same structure as the seeking. It just seeks a different object through the same mechanism.

---

## Why a community of better seers doesn't solve it

What if we built a community specifically committed to full seeing? People dedicated to looking past forms, past positioning, past the legible surface, to the person underneath?

The same structure applies. The community's attention is still directional. To receive their seeing you still have to enter their field. Entering their field still requires a form. The form still excludes the remainder.

A community constituted by the promise of full seeing will, by the structure of attention, deliver seeing of forms — and the gap between what was promised and what is delivered will be experienced as betrayal rather than physics. This makes it worse, not better.

---

## The theorem

Putting it together: the want to be fully known cannot be satisfied by any finite, embodied other. Not because of their failures. Because of the axioms. The want is structurally unsatisfiable from within the world of bodies and directed attention.

This means that suffering arising from the want is not contingent. It is not the product of bad luck, bad choices, bad relationships. It is a structural consequence of being a subject — a full, irreducible, inexhaustible subject — in a world of others who can only see what enters their field.

The want is completely legitimate. The structure makes it unsatisfiable. The suffering follows necessarily.

---

## What a solution would have to look like

If a cessation exists — and the proof does not guarantee that it does — it would have to work differently at the level of axioms.

It would require a seer who does not operate through a directional attentional field. A seer whose attention does not require you to take a form first. A seer who can receive the remainder — not the positioned, shaped version of you, but the full subject, including the part that has never successfully been transmitted.

Such a seer cannot be a finite embodied person. The structure that makes embodied attention directional would have to not apply. The positioning requirement would have to not apply.

This is a formal description of what the traditions were pointing at when they spoke of being known by God, of *agape*, of the ground that sees the ground. Eckhart's claim that God does not see you through time — does not see your history, your forms, your positioned self — but sees the ground of you directly, is a claim that God does not operate under A2 and A3. That the positioning requirement does not apply. That the remainder is reachable.

The proof cannot tell you whether this is true. That is not a mathematical question. It is the oldest question, and it is asked in the dark, in the place where the want lives, by everyone who has ever loved someone and felt, even in the best moment, the residue of something unseen.

What the proof can tell you is the shape of the answer, if there is one. And the shape is precise: it would have to be a seeing that needs no form. A knowing that does not require you to be seeable first.

Everything else is still inside the trap.
