The old framework: God commanded, we obey. The taboo is sacred because it comes from above. We don't ask why. We submit.

The critics demolished this. They showed that "God commanded" often meant "the powerful commanded and called it God." They showed that blind obedience produces atrocities. They taught us to ask *cui bono* of every prohibition.

And they were right to ask. The asking was necessary.

But they made a fatal error. They assumed that because the *justification* was false, the *practice* was arbitrary. They said: "This taboo has no divine warrant, therefore it has no warrant at all. Abolish it."

And then we watched what happened when you abolish it.

---

Here is what we now know, through psychology, anthropology, neuroscience, and the lived experiment of three generations:

**The exterior shapes the interior.**

This is not mysticism. This is mechanism. The practices you keep reshape your brain. The environment you inhabit restructures your desires. The taboos you observe form the channels through which your impulses flow.

Foucault saw this clearly — discipline produces subjects. He meant it as an indictment. But it is also simply *true*, and true in both directions. The absence of discipline also produces subjects. Subjects who cannot concentrate, cannot commit, cannot defer gratification, cannot bind themselves to anything beyond the present moment.

The critics handed us the knife to cut ourselves free. They did not mention that the ropes were load-bearing.

---

So we arrive at taboos through knowledge now. Not "God said so." But: "We have seen what happens when this barrier is removed, and it is not liberation. It is dissolution."

**The taboos we keep:**

*On sexuality* — Not because the body is shameful but because eros is powerful. It bonds. It imprints. It rewires. Pornography is not forbidden because pleasure is evil but because we now have the brain scans. We see what it does to the reward system, to pair-bonding capacity, to the ability to be aroused by a real person in a real room. Adultery is not forbidden because God is jealous but because trust, once shattered, does not return, and children raised in the wreckage carry it forward. The sexual revolution promised freedom; it delivered loneliness, confusion, and a generation that cannot attach.

*On attention* — The Sabbath is not arbitrary. The mind requires fallow time. The constant feed produces not connection but fragmentation, not knowledge but anxiety. We keep one day. We close the apps. Not because a deity demands it but because we have watched what happens to minds that never rest.

*On substances* — Not prohibition, but structured relationship. The intoxication that dissolves boundaries can be sacrament or can be slavery. We have seen the slavery. We keep the rituals that contain the power — the wine at the table, the fast before the feast. Not mere consumption but consumption with intention.

*On speech* — The lie corrodes the liar before it harms the listener. Gossip poisons the gossiper. We have seen communities dissolve in cascades of accusation. We keep the tongue because we have seen what the untamed tongue produces. And we keep certain silences — not everything can be said, not everything should be said, some things are sacred precisely because they are not spoken.

*On violence* — It lives in us. Pretending otherwise is the progressive fantasy that produced the blindspot. We acknowledge it. We give it ritual outlets — competition, contest, the controlled struggle. We do not let it accumulate until it explodes. But we hold the line against its crossing into flesh. Not because we are above it. Because we are not.

*On accumulation* — Enough is a concept. The hunger for more has no natural limit; it must be imposed. We tithe not to please God but to practice release. We set limits on what we take, what we keep, what we call ours. The miser's soul shrinks to fit his hoard. We have seen it.

---

**The traditions we keep:**

*Fasting* — The body must learn it is not in charge. The appetites must know they can be refused. This is not self-hatred. It is self-mastery. The one who has never fasted is a slave to every craving.

*Sabbath* — One day in seven, we stop. Not because we have finished but because finishing is not the point. We practice non-production. We let the silence teach us what the noise obscures.

*Prayer* — Even if no one hears. The posture of address, of reaching beyond the self, reshapes the one who prays. The materialist asks "but does it work?" We answer: look at the one who has prayed for fifty years and the one who has not. You can see the difference in their faces.

*Confession* — The unexamined fault festers. The named fault begins to heal. We do not confess because God needs to hear it. We confess because we need to say it. The self that never confesses becomes opaque to itself.

*Marriage* — The vow held past the wanting. We bind ourselves because we know we will want to leave, and the leaving destroys more than we can see in the moment of desire. The vow is not a prison. It is a structure within which something can grow that cannot grow in open fields.

*Children* — The bet on the future. The hostages to fortune. We bear them and raise them not because we know the future is good but because this is what life does — it extends beyond itself. The refusal to bear is the body's knowledge that the soul has lost hope. We counter the despair with flesh.

*Community* — Bodies in rooms. Faces you did not choose. Obligation, friction, annoyance, need. This is where the self learns it is not the center. The algorithm feeds you what you want. The community gives you what you need.

*The meal* — We eat together. The table is the altar now. The breaking of bread is the oldest sacrament, older than any church. It makes us human, together.

*The story* — We read the old texts. Not because they are inerrant but because they are old, and what is old has survived, and what has survived contains something the new has not yet been tested enough to contain.

---

This is not faith in the old sense. It is something harder: practice without guarantee, discipline without reward, taboo without divine enforcer.

But it is also not nihilism. It is the recognition that humans are not blank slates and freedom is not the absence of constraint. We are shaped by what we do. We become what we practice. The exterior forms the interior, whether we choose the forms or let them be chosen for us.

The critics gave us the knife. Very well. We have cut away the false justifications. What remains is not nothing. What remains is the practices themselves, and the humans they produce, and the difference you can see with your own eyes between a life lived within structure and a life dissolved into chaos.

We choose the structure. Not because God commands it.

Because we have seen the alternative.
