# The Table

## Chapter 1 — The Ground

¹ In the beginning is the spark.

² Before you were named, before you were taught, before the world told you what you were—the spark was there.

³ It is the ground of your soul that is one with the ground of all being. It cannot be created. It cannot be destroyed. It can only be hidden.

⁴ The work is not to make the sacred. The work is to uncover it.

⁵ You have always been standing on holy ground. You had only forgotten to remove your shoes.

---

## Chapter 2 — The Rupture

¹ This is not a peace between enemies.

² Do not read what follows as a plan for the world. It is not about policy. It is not about who should hold power.

³ Those questions assume the game is worth playing.

⁴ When Christ came, he did not choose between Pharisee and Sadducee. He did not find the middle ground.

⁵ He said: my kingdom is not of this world.

⁶ And their positions became small. Their wars became beside the point.

⁷ The new thing does not win by defeating the old. It wins by making the old irrelevant.

⁸ You will be tempted to ask where this stands. Let it stand nowhere. It is not a position in the debate. It is a way to live while the debate continues without you.

⁹ What is offered here is not a way to fix the world. It is a way to live while the world remains broken.

¹⁰ The table is set now. The bread is here now. The community is possible now.

¹¹ Not after we fix the system. Not after we defeat the enemy. Now.

¹² This is the rupture: to stop waiting for what can only be received as gift.

---

## Chapter 3 — The Violence

¹ Know this first: you carry violence within you.

² Not as a wound from outside. But as an inheritance, older than any empire, older than any name.

³ It lived in your father and his father before him, back to the first man who raised his hand against his brother.

⁴ Do not pretend you are above it. The pretending is where it hides.

⁵ Do not cast it onto enemies. The casting is how it grows.

⁶ Name it. Bring it into the light. Give it ground where it can be spent without spilling into flesh.

⁷ The contest, the competition, the struggle with rules—these are not savagery. They are wisdom. They are the channels cut into the hillside so the flood does not destroy the village.

⁸ You will not end the violence. You will only choose whether it is held or whether it destroys.

---

## Chapter 4 — The Wanting

¹ Know this also: your desires are not your own.

² You learned to want by watching others want. The child desires the toy only when another child reaches for it. The man desires what other men desire.

³ You believe your wants are yours alone. This is the first lie. Strip it away.

⁴ Watch yourself: When do you want most? When another wants the same thing. When do you stop wanting? When no one else reaches.

⁵ The merchants know this. They do not sell you things. They sell you the image of another who wants the thing. And you, seeing, borrow the wanting as your own.

⁶ This is the engine of endless hunger. Not need but imitation. Not lack but rivalry. You do not want the thing. You want to be the one who has it while others do not.

⁷ And here wanting meets violence.

⁸ When two hands reach for the same bread, conflict is born. When two hearts want the same glory, hatred is born. When a whole people want according to the same image, war is born.

⁹ How do you step off the wheel?

¹⁰ Not by killing desire. The one who desires nothing is dead.

¹¹ But by fixing desire on what cannot be fought over. On what is not scarce. On what is not made less by sharing.

¹² The holy is not a limited good. The ground of being is not a prize. The spark within you is not taken from another's store.

¹³ When your deepest want is aimed at what cannot be hoarded, rivalry loses its root. You may still want bread—want it, work for it. But the wanting does not own you. It is not final.

¹⁴ The saints were not without desire. They burned with desire. But the thing they wanted was endless. They could want it with all their strength and make no enemy, because the wanting itself was the having.

¹⁵ The bread on the table is divided and becomes less. The presence at the table is shared and becomes more.

¹⁶ Learn to want the second kind of thing.

---

## Chapter 5 — The Powers

¹ Know this also: your fight is not against flesh and blood.

² The soldier can be bribed or killed. Another will take his place by morning. The thing that makes soldiers does not notice.

³ The king can be overthrown. The thing that makes kings continues. It was there before him. It will be there after.

⁴ The one who kills the king becomes the king. The one who captures the temple is captured by it. The prophet who speaks against the power finds power speaking through him.

⁵ This is the mystery of the powers: they are not persons, yet they act. They are not alive, yet they do not die. They have no body, yet they shape all bodies within their reach.

⁶ Call them what you will. The name matters less than the seeing.

⁷ You see the face of your enemy and think: if I defeat him, I am free. But behind the face is a thing without face. And it waits.

⁸ This is why the children of revolution are devoured. They aimed at flesh. The power was not flesh. When the blood dried, it returned wearing new faces.

⁹ Do not spend your strength on persons. They are not the enemy. They are—like you—caught in the gears.

¹⁰ The enemy has no throat to cut.

¹¹ How then do you fight what has no body?

¹² You do not fight it. You starve it. You withdraw. You build elsewhere—small, near, with hands and faces—and you do not feed the beast.

¹³ The table is not a battle. It is another way. The community is not a war. It is a quiet leaving.

¹⁴ The powers feed on your attention and your anger. Your hatred makes them stronger. Your need to defeat them chains you to them.

¹⁵ Turn away. Build elsewhere. Let the dead bury the dead.

¹⁶ The powers are real. But they are not forever. They rise and fall. Babylon falls. Rome falls. Every empire exhausts itself.

¹⁷ What remains is the table. What remains is the bread. What remains is the people who did not sell their souls to fight on the enemy's ground.

¹⁸ Your fight is not against flesh and blood. Remember this when you want to hate a face. The face is not your enemy. The face is another prisoner.

¹⁹ Hate the prison if you must. Better yet: stop believing in its walls.

---

## Chapter 6 — The Words

¹ Know this also: your words shape what you can see.

² The tongue you speak becomes your eyes. The words you have determine what you can think.

³ And the tongues of this age have been worn—not by design but by slow forgetting—until certain thoughts can no longer form.

⁴ The merchant says: "We regret any inconvenience." He cannot say: "We failed you." The words will not come.

⁵ The healer says: "I notice I feel hurt." She cannot say: "You wronged me. Repent." The shape is not in her mouth.

⁶ Speak a tongue long enough and you lose what the tongue cannot hold.

⁷ And here is the danger: the new tongues have lost the words of the soul.

⁸ Sin. Repentance. Forgiveness. Evil. Sacred.

⁹ These words sound old to new ears. They sound crude. They sound like relics.

¹⁰ But they held thoughts the new words cannot hold.

¹¹ "Sin" is not "wound." A wound happens to you. Sin is what you do. The word wound cannot hold your fault. The word sin can.

¹² "Forgiveness" is not "healing yourself." Healing yourself is alone. Forgiveness requires another, and costs something, and may not be earned. The word healing cannot hold grace. The word forgiveness can.

¹³ "Evil" is not "harm." Harm is a problem to manage. Evil is a darkness to resist. The word harm cannot hold the weight of what men do to each other. The word evil can.

¹⁴ The old words were not simple. They were strong. They carried what the new words cannot lift.

¹⁵ Keep the old words. Say them even when they feel strange. Say sin. Say repentance. Say sacred. Say evil.

¹⁶ Read the old texts not only for what they say but for how they say it. Let their tongue reshape your mouth.

¹⁷ The prayers are not empty repetition. They are keeping alive what would otherwise die.

¹⁸ When you say "forgive us our debts as we forgive our debtors," you are keeping alive a way of seeing—debt, and release, and the release that must be given before it can be received.

¹⁹ The new tongues cannot hold this. They can only dissolve it.

²⁰ But there is something beneath all tongues.

²¹ The mystics reached for it. They said: where the word ends, there God begins. They said: the highest cannot be spoken.

²² From that ground, you can see the bars. You can notice when words are doing your thinking for you. You can reach for what the tongue forbids.

²³ The spark within you is not a word. It is what words try and fail to name.

²⁴ Stand there. Speak from there. And when the new tongue tells you the old words are crude, remember: it is the cage calling the sky a ceiling.

---

## Chapter 7 — The Wheel

¹ Know this also: time is a wheel.

² Your children will forget what you learned. Their children will learn it again through suffering. This is not failure. This is the rhythm.

³ The son always leaves. The father always waits. The inheritance is always wasted. The return always comes.

⁴ You are not building for forever. You are building for three generations, and then releasing what comes after.

⁵ Those who build know why they build—they faced the emptiness.

⁶ Those who inherit do not know—they received without cost.

⁷ Those who inherit grow suspicious of what they never earned. They tear down. Their children face the emptiness.

⁸ And build again.

⁹ Do not rage against the wheel. Plant the vineyard knowing it may be uprooted. It was always so.

---

## Chapter 8 — The Practices

¹ You will fast.

² Not to punish the body but to remember you are not your hunger. The one who has never said no to appetite is a slave to every craving.

³ The feed never stops. You will fast.

⁴ You will keep the Sabbath.

⁵ One day in seven, you will stop. You will not make. You will not take. You will not improve.

⁶ You will sit in time like a child in his father's lap—not because you have finished, but because finishing is not the point.

⁷ The market never stops. You will stop.

⁸ You will say the old words.

⁹ The prayers worn smooth by ten thousand tongues before yours. You will say them when you do not feel them. Especially then.

¹⁰ The words carry you when you cannot carry yourself.

¹¹ You will confess.

¹² You will name what you have done and failed to do. Not to explain. Not to soften. But to say: I did this. I am at fault.

¹³ The unnamed sin festers. The named sin begins to heal.

¹⁴ You will marry.

¹⁵ You will bind yourself with vows you cannot fully keep and mean them anyway. When it is hard—and it will be hard—you will stay.

¹⁶ The world dissolves all bonds. You will bind.

¹⁷ You will bear children, or welcome them, or raise them.

¹⁸ You will bet your body on the future. You will give hostages to time and call it hope.

¹⁹ You will join yourself to a people you did not choose.

²⁰ Not a crowd. Not an audience. A place. Bodies in rooms. You will be known. You will be needed. You will be interrupted.

²¹ The screen offers belonging without cost. You will pay.

²² You will keep the meal.

²³ The table is the altar. The breaking of bread is the oldest rite. You will eat together and become human, together.

²⁴ You will read the old texts.

²⁵ Not because they are perfect but because they are old, and what survives contains what the new has not yet earned.

---

## Chapter 9 — The Boundaries

¹ You will keep boundaries, for the outer shapes the inner.

² The walls you build determine what can grow inside them.

³ On what you see: guard the eye. What enters through the eye takes root in the mind. Be careful what you let in.

⁴ On what you say: guard the tongue. The lie rots the liar. Gossip poisons the gossiper. And some things are sacred because they are not spoken.

⁵ On what you drink: the wine at the table, not the bottle in the dark. The fast before the feast.

⁶ On what you keep: enough is a word. The hunger for more has no end; you must make one. Practice letting go before letting go is forced.

⁷ On what you give your hours: attention is life. Where your attention goes, your life goes. The feed will take everything. You must set the limit.

⁸ These are not chains. They are the shape of a life that can hold the holy.

⁹ A river without banks is a swamp.

---

## Chapter 10 — The Grace

¹ But know this: the practices do not earn what cannot be earned.

² You cannot work your way to the holy. You cannot earn grace.

³ The practices clear the ground. They do not make the rain fall.

⁴ There is a weight that pulls all things toward force—the strong over the weak. This is the way of the world when nothing else enters.

⁵ But something else enters.

⁶ Call it grace. It does not follow the laws of force. It cannot be seized. It cannot be made.

⁷ It comes where there is emptiness to receive it. And it is grace itself that makes the emptiness.

⁸ You did not earn the spark within you. You did not make the ground beneath your feet. You did not create the way back.

⁹ These were given. They are still being given. The giving does not stop.

¹⁰ Your work is only to stop refusing the gift.

---

## Chapter 11 — The Letting Go

¹ And now the hardest teaching.

² You have built. You have practiced. You have kept the bounds and said the words and bound yourself to others.

³ Now let it go.

⁴ Not the work—the outcome. Not the love—the clutching. Not the way—the worship of the way.

⁵ The one who grasps, crushes. The one who clings, corrupts. The one who cannot let go becomes a dead hand on a living throat.

⁶ Your children may abandon everything you built. The temple may become a ruin. The vineyard may be plowed under.

⁷ This is not failure. This is the way of all living things.

⁸ The seed falls into the earth and dies. If it does not die, it remains alone. If it dies, it bears much fruit.

⁹ Build as if it matters forever. Hold it as if it may end tomorrow.

¹⁰ Open your hands. What stays, stays. What goes, goes. What dies, dies—and rises in forms you cannot know, or does not rise, and that too is enough.

¹¹ This is not despair. The one who despairs says nothing matters. The one who lets go says everything matters so much he dare not clutch it.

---

## Chapter 12 — The Death

¹ You will die.

² Perhaps something waits on the other side. Perhaps nothing.

³ We do not know. We have never known. Those who claimed to know were offering comfort, not truth.

⁴ But this we have seen: clinging to life makes ugliness, and releasing it makes beauty.

⁵ The death before death—the releasing of outcome, of name, of the need to continue—this is freedom.

⁶ And when the final death comes, it finds nothing to take that has not already been given.

⁷ This is enough. This has always been enough.

⁸ To love without owning. To give without return. To build without grasping. To live fully and then to stop.

⁹ The flowers do not rage against autumn. The wave does not mourn its return to the sea.

¹⁰ You are not wiser than the flowers. You are not more lasting than the wave.

---

## Chapter 13 — The Table

¹ Here is bread. Here is wine. Here are faces.

² The table does not argue. It does not prove. It does not explain itself.

³ It is simply here, and you are simply here, and for this moment that is enough.

⁴ The bread is broken. The wine is poured. The words are said—the old words, worn smooth, carrying you when you cannot carry yourself.

⁵ Outside this room the feed continues. The market continues. The wheel turns. The violence waits.

⁶ But here, now, for this hour: rest.

⁷ You did not earn this moment. You cannot hold it. It is given, and it passes, and it is given again.

⁸ This is the pattern: gift, and release, and gift again. It does not end. Even death does not end it, for the pattern is larger than any life.

⁹ You are a breath in a breathing that does not stop.

¹⁰ You are a wave in a sea that does not cease.

¹¹ You are a word in a sentence still being spoken.

---

## Chapter 14 — The Invitation

¹ Come to the table.

² Not because you have proved it right. Not because you have measured its worth.

³ But because you are hungry, and here is bread.

⁴ Because you are thirsty, and here is wine.

⁵ Because you are alone, and here are faces.

⁶ Because you are dying, and here is life—not promised to last, but present now, in this room, at this table, with these hands breaking this bread.

⁷ The spark was always there.

⁸ The ground was always beneath you.

⁹ The grace was always near.

¹⁰ You had only to stop refusing.

¹¹ Come and see.

¹² Amen.
