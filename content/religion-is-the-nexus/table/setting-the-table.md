# The Turn

For two years I have been writing essays. Essays about the left and the right, about the genealogy of power, about perception gaps and institutional capture and the dissolution of meaning. I wrote about what the progressives did to the conservatives, what the deconstructors did to the tradition, what the therapists and platforms did to the soul. I named the beasts. I traced the bloodlines. I showed my work.

Underneath all of it was an assumption I never questioned: that the goal was synthesis. Healing. Reconciliation. I imagined myself standing between two warring camps, translating each to the other, finding the common ground they had forgotten they shared. If I could just show the left what they looked like to the right, if I could just show the right their own genealogy, if I could just get everyone to see clearly — then perhaps the fracture could be mended.

I thought I was doing what Jesus did.

I was wrong.

---

Jesus did not come to synthesize the Pharisees and the Sadducees. He did not split the difference between the Zealots and the Herodians. He did not offer a "third way" that made everyone feel heard.

He said: *I did not come to bring peace, but a sword.*

He said: *I have come to set a man against his father, and a daughter against her mother.*

He said: *Let the dead bury their own dead. Follow me.*

The Kingdom he proclaimed was not a reconciliation of existing positions. It was an entirely different axis breaking into history — one that made the old divisions suddenly irrelevant while creating a new and more fundamental one. Those who enter, and those who don't.

This is not synthesis. This is rupture. This is a new thing that judges the old things by its mere presence, not by argument.

---

I look back at my essays now and I see their shape more clearly.

*The Violence You Fear* — here is what you are getting wrong, here is the data, here is the mirror.

*The Epistle to the Managed* — here are the beasts, here are the cages, here is the naming.

*The Return* — here is the fall, here is the critique of the critique, here is the synthesis that comes after.

Even *The Return*, which I thought was constructive, is still framed against what came before. It is a *return* — defined by the departure it reverses. Part I diagnoses. Part II offers practices. But the practices are presented as responses to the void, not as goods in themselves.

The prophet points at the Pharisees and cries *brood of vipers*. 

The teacher says *follow me* and walks toward Galilee.

Both are necessary. But only one founds the church.

---

There is a kind of writing that argues people out of error. It assumes the reader is wrong and needs correction. It traces genealogies, exposes hidden assumptions, reveals the man behind the curtain. This writing is important. It clears ground. It names what must be named.

But there is another kind of writing. It assumes the reader already half-wants something and just needs to see it embodied. It does not argue against the old world; it invites into a new one. It does not say *here is why your position is false*; it says *here is a table, here is bread, come and eat*.

The first kind of writing creates conviction through demolition.

The second creates conviction through presence.

I have done a great deal of demolition. I have named the beasts. I have traced the bloodlines. I have held up mirrors.

Perhaps it is time to set a table.

---

What does this mean in practice?

It means writing that describes the texture of a well-lived day rather than the pathology of a poorly-lived one.

It means portraits of people and communities who are already doing it — not as arguments, but as invitations.

It means less *you* and more *we*. Less accusation, more assumption of shared longing.

It means writing that does not need to defeat the alternatives because it is too busy being beautiful to bother.

The new thing does not win by proving the old thing wrong. It wins by being so compelling that the old thing is simply — forgotten. Rendered irrelevant. Left behind like a snake's shed skin.

---

I do not know if I can write this way. Diagnosis is easier. Critique is easier. There is a satisfaction in naming what is wrong — a clarity, a righteous pleasure. To build is harder. To invite is harder. It requires believing that something good is actually there, actually available, actually worth inhabiting.

But I think this is where the work goes next.

Not because the prophetic work was wrong. It was necessary. Someone must name the beasts. Someone must clear the ground.

But the ground has been cleared. Or cleared enough.

Now: what will we build on it? What does the city look like? What does the day look like? What does the meal taste like, the prayer feel like, the community sound like?

These are the questions that remain. And they cannot be answered by pointing at what is broken.

They can only be answered by showing what is whole.

---

So I am turning. Not away from what I have written — it stands, it was true, it did its work. But toward something else now. Toward the positive content of the life I have been defending in the negative.

The sword divides. That is its nature and its purpose.

But after the division comes the question: *What are we divided for? What is on this side of the line that makes the line worth drawing?*

I want to answer that question now. Not by arguing. By showing.

Come and see.
