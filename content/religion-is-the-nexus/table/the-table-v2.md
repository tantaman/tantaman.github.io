# The Table

## Chapter 1 — The Ground

¹ In the beginning is the spark.

² Before you were named, before you were taught, before the world told you what you were—the spark was there.

³ It is the ground of your soul that is one with the ground of all being. It cannot be created. It cannot be destroyed. It can only be obscured.

⁴ The work is not to manufacture the sacred. The work is to clear away what hides it.

⁵ You have always been standing on holy ground. You had only forgotten to remove your shoes.

---

## Chapter 2 — The Rupture

¹ This is not a synthesis.

² Do not read what follows as the reconciliation of left and right, the healing of the partisan divide, the third way that finally brings the warring tribes together.

³ It is not Hegel's march of history arriving at its destination. It is not thesis and antithesis resolving into higher unity. It is not progress.

⁴ The left says: liberation from oppressive structures. The right says: preservation of inherited wisdom. And the dialectician says: let us find what is true in both.

⁵ But this is not that.

⁶ This is a rupture. A different axis breaking into the plane where left and right conduct their endless war.

⁷ When Christ came, he did not adjudicate between Pharisee and Sadducee. He did not find the middle ground between Zealot and Herodian. He did not synthesize.

⁸ He said: my kingdom is not of this world.

⁹ And in saying so, he made their positions—all of them—suddenly provincial. Suddenly small. Suddenly beside the point.

¹⁰ The new thing does not win by defeating the old positions. It wins by making them irrelevant.

¹¹ You will be tempted to place what follows on the spectrum. To say: this is conservative, or this is progressive, or this is the best of both.

¹² Resist the temptation. The spectrum is a cage. Step out of it.

¹³ What is offered here is not a political program. It is not a policy platform. It will not tell you how to vote or whom to support.

¹⁴ It is a way of living that does not require the political to be resolved. That does not wait for the right party to win. That does not postpone the good life until the revolution comes or the restoration arrives.

¹⁵ The table is set now. The bread is here now. The community is possible now.

¹⁶ Not after we fix the system. Not after we defeat the enemy. Now.

¹⁷ This is the rupture: to stop waiting for history to deliver what can only be received as gift.

---

## Chapter 3 — The Violence

¹ Know this first: you carry violence within you.

² Not as a corruption from outside. Not as a wound inflicted by systems. But as a inheritance, older than any empire, older than any name.

³ The will to power lives in your chest as it lived in your father's chest and his father's before him, back to the first man who raised his hand against his brother.

⁴ Do not pretend you are above it. The pretending is where it hides.

⁵ Do not project it onto enemies. The projection is how it grows.

⁶ Name it. Bring it into the light. Give it ritual ground where it can be spent without spilling into flesh.

⁷ The contest, the competition, the controlled struggle—these are not barbarism. They are wisdom. They are the channels cut into the hillside so the flood does not destroy the village.

⁸ You will not eliminate the violence. You will only choose whether it is contained or catastrophic.

---

## Chapter 4 — The Mimesis

¹ Know this also: your desires are not your own.

² You learned to want by watching others want. The child desires the toy only when another child reaches for it. The adult desires the career, the spouse, the status, because others desire them.

³ This is mimesis. It is as deep in you as violence. It is older than language.

⁴ You believe your desires are original, authentic, yours. This is the first illusion. Strip it away.

⁵ Watch yourself carefully: When do you want most intensely? When another wants the same thing. When do you lose interest? When no one else competes.

⁶ The marketing men know this. They do not sell you products. They sell you the image of another who desires the product. And you, mimetic creature that you are, borrow the desire as if it were your own.

⁷ This is the engine of endless accumulation. Not need but imitation. Not hunger but rivalry. You do not want the thing. You want to be the one who has it while others do not.

⁸ And here mimesis meets violence.

⁹ For when two hands reach for the same object, conflict is born. When two hearts want the same glory, enmity is born. When a whole society wants according to the same model, war is born.

¹⁰ The scapegoat emerges from mimetic crisis. When the rivalry becomes unbearable, the crowd turns on one. They unite in violence against the one. Peace is restored—temporarily. Until the cycle begins again.

¹¹ This is the hidden structure beneath your politics, your markets, your social frenzies. Mimetic desire, mimetic rivalry, mimetic violence, mimetic peace. Round and round.

¹² How do you step off the wheel?

¹³ Not by purging desire. The one who desires nothing is dead.

¹⁴ But by grounding desire in what cannot be competed for. In what is not scarce. In what is not diminished by sharing.

¹⁵ The transcendent is not a limited good. The ground of being is not a prize. The spark within you is not taken from another's store.

¹⁶ When your deepest desire is aimed at what cannot be hoarded, rivalry loses its foundation. You may still want bread—want it, compete for it if you must. But the wanting does not own you. It is not ultimate.

¹⁷ The saints were not desireless. They burned with desire. But the object of their desire was inexhaustible. They could want it with all their strength and create no rival, because the want itself was the having.

¹⁸ Guard against mimetic desire. Not by refusing to want. But by wanting what cannot be stolen, what cannot be lost, what cannot be diminished by another's possession.

¹⁹ The bread on the table is divided and becomes less. The presence at the table is shared and becomes more.

²⁰ Learn to want the second kind of thing.

## Chapter 5 — The Principalities

¹ Know this also: your struggle is not against flesh and blood.

² The tax man can be bribed or killed. Another will take his place by morning. The system that produces tax men does not notice the loss.

³ The politician can be voted out, overthrown, assassinated. The structure that produces politicians continues. It was there before him. It will be there after.

⁴ The revolutionary kills the king and becomes the king. The reformer captures the institution and is captured by it. The prophet speaks against the power and finds power speaking through him.

⁵ This is the mystery of the principalities: they are not persons, yet they act. They are not alive, yet they persist. They have no body, yet they shape all bodies within their reach.

⁶ Call them systems. Call them structures. Call them the rulers of the darkness of this age. The name matters less than the seeing.

⁷ The Prince of Persia opposed the angel for twenty-one days. Not a man—a power. Not flesh—a pattern. Not blood—a momentum that carries flesh and blood along.

⁸ You see the face of your enemy and think: if I defeat him, I am free. But behind the face is a structure, and behind the structure is another structure, and behind that is something that has no face at all.

⁹ The corporation is not its CEO. Remove him; the corporation continues. The state is not its president. Remove her; the state continues. The ideology is not its spokesman. Silence him; the ideology continues.

¹⁰ This is why revolution devours its children. The children aimed at flesh. The principality was not flesh. It waited, and when the blood dried, it returned wearing new faces.

¹¹ Do not waste your violence on persons. They are not the enemy. They are—like you—caught in the gears.

¹² The enemy has no throat to cut. This is its advantage and its nature.

¹³ How then do you fight what has no body?

¹⁴ You do not fight it. You starve it. You withdraw your attention, your participation, your belief. You build structures of your own—small, local, embodied—that do not feed the principality.

¹⁵ The table is not a battle. It is an alternative. The community is not a revolution. It is a quiet secession.

¹⁶ The principalities feed on your attention and your outrage. Your hatred of them makes them stronger. Your obsession with defeating them chains you to them.

¹⁷ Turn away. Build elsewhere. Let the dead bury the dead.

¹⁸ The powers are real. But they are not ultimate. They rise and fall. Babylon falls. Rome falls. The corporation dissolves. The ideology exhausts itself.

¹⁹ What remains is the table. What remains is the bread. What remains is the community that did not sell its soul to fight on the principality's terms.

²⁰ Your struggle is not against flesh and blood. Remember this when you are tempted to hate a face. The face is not your enemy. The face is another prisoner.

²¹ Hate the prison if you must. Better yet: stop believing in its walls.

---

## Chapter 6 — The Language

¹ Know this also: your language is not neutral.

² The words you speak shape the thoughts you can think. The grammar you use determines what can be assembled in your mind.

³ This is not metaphor. It is mechanism. Speak a language long enough and it becomes your perception. The categories it provides become the categories of your world.

⁴ And the grammars of this age have been crafted—not by conspiracy but by slow evolution—to disable certain thoughts.

⁵ The professional grammar says: "We should explore performance improvement opportunities." It cannot say: "Fire him. He is incompetent." The thought will not assemble.

⁶ The therapeutic grammar says: "I'm noticing I feel hurt by what happened." It cannot say: "You wronged me. Apologize." Accusation is not a move the grammar permits.

⁷ The commercial grammar says: "We apologize for any inconvenience this may have caused." It cannot say: "We failed you. We are responsible." Admission is outside its structure.

⁸ These are not choices of politeness. They are constraints on cognition. Speak the grammar long enough and you lose the ability to think the forbidden thought.

⁹ The woman who speaks only therapy-speak does not bite her tongue when she wants to accuse. She has lost the concept of accusation. "You wronged me" sounds to her like violence, like pathology, like someone who has not done the work.

¹⁰ The man who speaks only corporate-speak does not hide his demands behind soft words. He has lost the concept of demand. "I want a raise or I will leave" sounds to him crude, unsophisticated, unprofessional.

¹¹ The grammar has become their mind. The cage is complete because they cannot see the bars.

¹² And here is the deeper danger: the new grammars have disabled the language of the soul.

¹³ Sin. Repentance. Forgiveness. Righteousness. Evil. Sacred. Profane.

¹⁴ These words sound archaic to modern ears. They sound unsophisticated. They sound like relics of a primitive age.

¹⁵ But they encoded thoughts that the new grammars cannot think.

¹⁶ "Sin" is not "trauma." Trauma happens to you. Sin is what you do. The grammar of trauma cannot hold responsibility. The grammar of sin can.

¹⁷ "Forgiveness" is not "processing" or "closure." Processing is something you do alone, for yourself. Forgiveness requires another, and costs something, and may not be deserved. The grammar of processing cannot hold grace. The grammar of forgiveness can.

¹⁸ "Evil" is not "harmful behavior." Harmful behavior is a technical problem to be managed. Evil is a moral reality to be resisted. The grammar of harm cannot hold the weight of what humans do to each other. The grammar of evil can.

¹⁹ The old words were not primitive. They were load-bearing. They carried thoughts the species needed to think.

²⁰ And now they are falling out of use, and the thoughts are becoming unthinkable, and we wonder why we feel confused, weightless, unable to name what is happening to us.

²¹ Therefore: guard your grammar as you guard your eyes.

²² Keep the old words. Say them even when they feel archaic. Say sin. Say repentance. Say sacred. Say evil.

²³ Read the old texts not just for their content but for their grammar. Let their syntax reshape your mind. Let their vocabulary re-enable thoughts the new languages have disabled.

²⁴ The liturgy is not nostalgia. It is cognitive preservation. The creed is not rote. It is a grammar that keeps certain thoughts thinkable.

²⁵ When you say "I believe in God the Father Almighty," you are not merely expressing an opinion. You are keeping alive a grammar in which fatherhood and almightiness can be thought together. In which dependence and sovereignty can be held in one frame.

²⁶ When you say "forgive us our trespasses as we forgive those who trespass against us," you are keeping alive a grammar of mutual obligation, of moral debt, of release that must be given before it can be received.

²⁷ The new grammars cannot hold these thoughts. They can only dissolve them into therapeutic process or contractual obligation.

²⁸ And here is the final point: the transcendent is what lets you see the grammar from outside.

²⁹ Every grammar is a cage. Every language constrains even as it enables. There is no grammar-free speech, no view from nowhere.

³⁰ But there is a ground beneath all grammars. The mystics reached for it. They said: where the word ends, there God begins. They said: the highest cannot be spoken.

³¹ This is not obscurantism. It is precision. The transcendent exceeds every grammar because it is the ground from which all grammars arise.

³² And from that ground, you can see the bars. You can notice when a grammar is doing your thinking for you. You can reach for words the grammar forbids.

³³ The grammar is a principality. It has no body. It cannot be killed. It acts through you without your consent.

³⁴ But you have access to what exceeds it. The spark within you is not a grammar. It is what grammars try and fail to name.

³⁵ Stand there. Speak from there. And when the new grammar tells you the old words are primitive, remember: it is the cage calling the sky a ceiling.

## Chapter 7 — The Cycle

¹ Know this also: time is a wheel.

² Your children will forget what you have learned. Their children will learn it again through suffering. This is not failure. This is the rhythm.

³ The prodigal always leaves. The father always waits. The inheritance is always squandered. The return always comes.

⁴ You are not building for permanence. You are building for the next three generations, and then releasing what comes after.

⁵ Those who build know why they build—they faced the void.

⁶ Those who inherit do not know—they received without cost.

⁷ Those who inherit grow suspicious of what they never had to fight for. They tear down. Their children face the void.

⁸ And build again.

⁹ Do not rage against the cycle. You are not wiser than ten thousand generations. Plant the vineyard knowing it may be uprooted. It was always so.

## Chapter 8 — The Practices

¹ You will fast.

² Not to punish the body but to remember you are not your appetites. The one who has never said no to hunger is a slave to every craving.

³ The feed never stops. You will fast.

⁴ You will keep the Sabbath.

⁵ One day in seven, you will stop. You will not produce. You will not consume. You will not improve.

⁶ You will sit in time like a child in his father's lap—not because you have finished, but because finishing is not the point.

⁷ The market never stops. You will stop.

⁸ You will say the old words.

⁹ The prayers worn smooth by ten thousand tongues before yours. You will say them when you do not feel them. Especially then.

¹⁰ The words carry you when you cannot carry yourself.

¹¹ You will confess.

¹² You will name what you have done and failed to do. Not to therapize. Not to contextualize. But to say: I did this. I am responsible.

¹³ The unexamined fault festers. The named fault begins to heal.

¹⁴ You will marry.

¹⁵ You will bind yourself with vows you cannot fully keep and mean them anyway. When it is hard—and it will be hard—you will stay.

¹⁶ The liquid world dissolves all bonds. You will bind.

¹⁷ You will bear children, or welcome them, or raise them.

¹⁸ You will bet your body on the future. You will send hostages to fortune and call it hope.

¹⁹ You will join yourself to a community you did not choose.

²⁰ Not a network. Not an audience. A place. A people. Bodies in rooms. You will be known and be obligated. You will be interrupted, annoyed, needed.

²¹ The screen offers connection without cost. You will pay.

²² You will keep the meal.

²³ The table is the altar. The breaking of bread is the oldest sacrament. You will eat together and be made human, together.

²⁴ You will read the old texts.

²⁵ Not because they are inerrant but because they are old, and what survives contains what the new has not yet been tested enough to hold.

---

## Chapter 9 — The Boundaries

¹ You will keep boundaries, for the exterior shapes the interior.

² The channels you cut determine where the water flows. The walls you build determine what can grow inside them.

³ On what you see: guard the eye. What enters through the eye takes root in the mind. The image that arouses without relationship rewires the capacity for relationship. Be careful what you let in.

⁴ On what you say: guard the tongue. The lie corrodes the liar. Gossip poisons the gossiper. And some things are sacred precisely because they are not spoken.

⁵ On what you consume: the wine at the table, not the bottle in the dark. The fast before the feast. Intoxication with intention, not obliteration.

⁶ On what you accumulate: enough is a concept. The hunger for more has no natural limit; you must impose one. Practice release before release is forced upon you.

⁷ On what you give your hours: attention is the substance of life. Where your attention goes, there your life goes. The feed will take everything. You must set limits the feed will not set for you.

⁸ These are not arbitrary restrictions. They are the shape of a life that can hold the sacred.

⁹ A river without banks is a swamp.

---

## Chapter 10 — The Grace

¹ But know this: the practices do not earn what cannot be earned.

² You cannot technique your way to the holy. You cannot discipline yourself into grace.

³ The practices clear the ground. They do not make the rain fall.

⁴ There is a gravity that pulls all things toward force, toward power, toward the strong dominating the weak. This is the way of the world when nothing else intervenes.

⁵ But something else intervenes.

⁶ Call it grace. It does not obey the laws of force. It cannot be seized. It cannot be manufactured.

⁷ It enters where there is emptiness to receive it. And strangely, it is grace itself that creates the emptiness.

⁸ You did not earn the spark within you. You did not manufacture the ground on which you stand. You did not create the possibility of your own redemption.

⁹ These were given. They are still being given. The giving does not stop.

¹⁰ Your work is only to stop refusing the gift.

---

## Chapter 11 — The Releasement

¹ And now the hardest teaching.

² You have built. You have practiced. You have kept the boundaries and said the words and bound yourself to others.

³ Now let it go.

⁴ Not the work—the outcome. Not the love—the clutching. Not the tradition—the idolatry of the tradition.

⁵ The one who grasps, crushes. The one who clings, corrupts. The one who cannot release becomes a dead hand on a living throat.

⁶ Your children may abandon everything you built. The church may become a ruin. The vineyard may be bulldozed.

⁷ This is not failure. This is the way of all living things.

⁸ The seed falls into the earth and dies. If it does not die, it remains alone. If it dies, it bears much fruit.

⁹ Build as if it matters forever. Hold it as if it may end tomorrow.

¹⁰ Open your hands. What stays, stays. What goes, goes. What dies, dies—and rises in forms you cannot predict, or does not rise, and that too is acceptable.

¹¹ The mystic prayed: God, rid me of God. Rid me of my small container that I have mistaken for the sea.

¹² This is not nihilism. The nihilist says nothing matters. The mystic says everything matters so much he dare not grasp it.

---

## Chapter 12 — The Death

¹ You will die.

² Perhaps something waits on the other side. Perhaps nothing.

³ We do not know. We have never known. The traditions that claimed certainty were offering comfort, not knowledge.

⁴ But this we have seen: clinging to life produces ugliness, and releasing it produces beauty.

⁵ The death before death—the releasing of outcome, of reputation, of the need to continue—this is freedom.

⁶ And when the final death comes, it finds nothing to take that has not already been released.

⁷ This is enough. This has always been enough.

⁸ To love without possessing. To give without return. To build without owning. To live fully and then to stop.

⁹ The flowers do not rage against autumn. The wave does not mourn its return to the sea.

¹⁰ You are not wiser than the flowers. You are not more permanent than the wave.

---

## Chapter 13 — The Table

¹ Come to the table.

² Here is bread. Here is wine. Here are faces.

³ The table does not argue. It does not prove. It does not explain itself.

⁴ It is simply here, and you are simply here, and for this moment that is enough.

⁵ The bread is broken. The wine is poured. The words are said—the old words, worn smooth, carrying you when you cannot carry yourself.

⁶ Outside this room the feed continues. The market continues. The cycle turns. The violence waits.

⁷ But here, now, for this hour: rest.

⁸ You did not earn this moment. You cannot clutch it. It is given, and it passes, and it is given again.

⁹ This is the pattern: gift, and release, and gift again. It does not end. Even death does not end it, for the pattern is larger than any single life.

¹⁰ You are a breath in a breathing that does not stop.

¹¹ You are a wave in a sea that does not cease.

¹² You are a word in a sentence still being spoken.

---

## Chapter 14 — The Invitation

¹ Come to the table.

² Not because you have proven it is right. Not because you have calculated that it works.

³ But because you are hungry, and here is bread.

⁴ Because you are thirsty, and here is wine.

⁵ Because you are alone, and here are faces.

⁶ Because you are dying, and here is life—not guaranteed to continue, but present now, in this room, at this table, with these hands breaking this bread.

⁷ The spark was always there.

⁸ The ground was always beneath you.

⁹ The grace was always available.

¹⁰ You had only to stop refusing.

¹¹ Come and see.

¹² Amen.
