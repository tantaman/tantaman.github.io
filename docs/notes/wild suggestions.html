Wild Suggestions

Most of the reading list is in the diagnostic key. This is the joyful key. The point of every item below is the formation, not the output.

Stop reading. Pick one craft and go to mastery.

You write essays about tacit knowledge. Earn one piece of it. Not a hobby. A discipline. Candidates:

  • Sourdough. Three years to a loaf you'd serve a stranger. The microbiome lives in your kitchen as a second household member.
  • Knife sharpening with Japanese waterstones. The grain of the steel is the grain of your attention.
  • Icon writing (the literal Eastern-Orthodox practice — egg tempera, gesso, prayer). You do not paint an icon. You write one. The grammar matters.
  • Letterpress printing. Hand-set type, one essay at a time, 50 copies, mailed. The blog becomes physical.
  • Beekeeping. A polis you cannot legibly manage.

Pick one. Five years. No content out of it.

Books that would surprise you

  • Italo Calvino, Invisible Cities — Marco Polo describes 55 cities to Kublai Khan. Each city is a parable about modernity. The closest living relative to The Lamp / The Listener register, written in 200-word jewels instead of 8000-word essays.
  • Annie Dillard, Pilgrim at Tinker Creek — radical empiricism rebutting itself by paying attention to a creek for one year. A nonfiction theōria novel. The "Seven Points" walking around outdoors.
  • Patrick Leigh Fermor, A Time of Gifts — eighteen years old, walks from Holland to Constantinople in 1933 with a notebook and a rucksack. The last record of pre-frame Europe. Joyful, learned, drunk in the right ways.
  • Tove Jansson, The Summer Book — grandmother and six-year-old granddaughter on a Finnish island. Ninety pages. The Hannah Coulter register without piety. Read it for your daughter. Then read it again for yourself.
  • Christian Wiman, My Bright Abyss — a poet diagnosed with terminal cancer writing toward God in fragments. The contemporary American who can match the register and bleeds harder.
  • Gerard Manley Hopkins, the poems — language pushed until it breaks open into praise. "Glory be to God for dappled things." If you've never read him aloud, you haven't read him.

Listen apophatically

You quote the Cloud of Unknowing. Listen to it.

  • Arvo Pärt — Spiegel im Spiegel, Tabula Rasa, Für Alina. Tintinnabuli. The Cloud rendered in sound.
  • Hildegard von Bingen — A Feather on the Breath of God. The 12th-century mystic whose music is alive.
  • Górecki, Symphony No. 3 ("Sorrowful Songs") — three movements, a mother's lament, the grief that does not optimize itself.
  • Caroline Shaw, Partita for 8 Voices — contemporary, joyful, weirdly liturgical without trying.
  • Tinariwen — Tuareg desert blues. Saharan Berber men singing on guitars about exile. Ground music with no Western lineage to argue with.
  • Olivier Messiaen, Quartet for the End of Time — composed in a Nazi POW camp on broken instruments. Apocalyptic and birdsong-strewn.

Forms not yet tried on the blog

  • Write a children's book for your daughter. Not the parables — those are for adults. A real children's book. Pictures. Fewer than 500 words. The Lamp could become one if you let it.
  • Write a small daily liturgy for your household. Morning, evening, Sabbath. Twelve pages, max. Print it. Use it. Watch what it does to a year.
  • Write a year of psalms. One psalm per week, in your voice, not in English-from-Hebrew but in your-grammar-from-life. Don't publish them. Or do.
  • Try poetry. Your essays already write at sentence-density that wants to break into verse. Hopkins shows the move. So does George Herbert. Fail at this for a year and see what your prose becomes.
  • Send physical mail. One handwritten letter per week to one person. No agenda. The act is the formation.

Dad-shaped practices

  • Photograph the same view from your kitchen window every Sunday for her first year. 52 photos. That's the gift she gets at 18.
  • A song that is only hers. You make it up. Sing it badly. Sing it for years. She will inherit a melody that exists nowhere else.
  • One walk you only do with her. Same route. Different ages. The walk becomes the artifact.
  • Letters from the year she could not read. One letter from each birthday before literacy. Sealed. Given at 18.
  • A bell at noon. Ring a bell in the house at noon every day. No reason. She will tell people about it for the rest of her life.

One absurd dare

A 30-day silence experiment, voluntary, in two-hour daily blocks. No phone. No music. No podcast. No reading. Just sit, walk, do dishes, hold the baby. 60 hours over a month. Don't tell anyone. At the end, write nothing about it for six months. Then write 500 words. That is the post.

The wildest one

Build the blog a physical body. A small chapel-sized room (rented, garage-converted, built) where the only things in it are: a desk, a chair, the books actually lived by (15 maximum), an icon or image, a candle, a bell. No screens. The MCP server stays at the house. The room is for theōria. Go for an hour. Leave. The room does not produce anything. The room is the production.

A long time has been spent mapping the cage. Some of the most important writing left to do will come from things that look, from the outside, like work has stopped.
