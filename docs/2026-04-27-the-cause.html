The left's shortcut, and why it fails worse than the right's.

Call her Sarah. She is twenty-six, a PhD candidate in comparative literature at Columbia, raised secular in Westchester, no synagogue, no church, no extended family within driving distance, no neighborhood she knows by name, no political affiliation until two years ago. October 7 happens. She watches the response. By April 2024 she is at the encampment on the South Lawn. For the first time in her life she is part of a movement. She has a uniform — keffiyeh, mask — a function (legal observer, food prep), a future (the cause), and an enemy. She joins. She tells her parents, the next time she sees them, that she has finally found herself. The sentence is true at the level of her experience. It is also exactly the offer the apparatus had prepared.

The other essay traced this architecture on its right wing. Mass population without formation. Schmitt, Dugin, Karp — three sovereign-decision answers across a century, each correctly diagnosing the dissolution and reaching for a regime to fill it. Mobilization is fast, formation is slow, the regime offers what feels like belonging while the polity that requires belonging continues to dissolve.

This essay traces the same architecture on its left wing.

The Original Formation

Marxism was supposed to be the answer to the formation problem, not its mirror.

When liberalism was new it could afford to be neutral on formation because the formation was happening elsewhere — parish, household, guild, regiment, the whole inheritance from the order liberalism replaced. The left's claim, from the beginning, was different. Marxism was not procedural-neutral. It was a substantive alternative. It proposed a new substrate, produced by new institutions, formed in new disciplines. The party. The cell. The collective. The union hall. The workers' institute. The cooperative. The kibbutz. These were not auxiliary to left politics. They were the politics. The point was not to extend rights to atomized individuals. It was to produce a different kind of person.

Lenin understood this with more clarity than any other early Marxist. What Is To Be Done?, 1902, is a formation manual disguised as a strategy document. Mass spontaneity produces strikes, not revolutions. Revolutions require formed people — disciplined cadres, professional revolutionaries, a party that does not reflect the consciousness of its members but produces it. The Bolsheviks won because they had cadre formation. The Mensheviks lost because they trusted spontaneity. Lenin won an argument the contemporary left has forgotten he was even having.

The kibbutz at Degania, founded 1909 on the southern shore of the Sea of Galilee, was the lived form of the same insight. Ten Russian Jewish settlers, drawing on Tolstoyan and socialist commitments, organized communal agriculture under a discipline that approached monastic intensity. Communal property. Communal childcare. Communal deliberation. Daily labor. Religious-style ritual applied to secular socialist commitments. By the 1970s the kibbutzim were producing forty percent of Israeli agricultural output and a wildly disproportionate share of military and political leadership. They were a formation system. The politics followed from the formation, as Marx had predicted, except the formation had to be deliberately built — Marx had thought the industrial mode of production would build it automatically, and it didn't.

The American case had its own form. The Industrial Workers of the World — the Wobblies — at their peak ran a structure that combined union, fraternal society, and political school. The Catholic Worker movement, founded 1933 by Dorothy Day and Peter Maurin, ran houses of hospitality on a discipline of voluntary poverty and daily Mass. The Communist Party USA in the 1930s, before it was crushed, ran reading groups, summer camps, marriage counseling, sports leagues, mutual aid funds. These institutions formed people. Then the people did the politics.

The left, in this period, was more formation-heavy than liberalism, not less. Its commitments were maximal. It needed more from its members than liberalism needed from its citizens. The cell answer to the post-substrate population was, originally, a left answer.

The Hollowing

It died on a long timeline.

Taft-Hartley, 1947, purged communists from American unions, severing the political-formation tradition from the labor-formation tradition. The unions survived; their formation function did not. By the 1970s the American union was a wage-bargaining apparatus, not a formation institution.

Khrushchev's secret speech, 1956, broke a generation of left intellectuals from the Party. The institutional formation system in the West contracted to a fraction of its prewar size. Theory began to detach from practice because the practice's institutional substrate was eroding.

1968 was the decisive year. The new left was born in universities. It was theory-heavy from the start. It never built formation infrastructure to replace what had been lost. After 1968, left politics in the West is increasingly conducted as discourse rather than formation. The seminar replaces the union hall. The journal replaces the cell. Reading replaces practice. By the time the actual material formation institutions — unions, cooperatives, mutual aid societies — finished dying in the 1980s and 1990s, the new left had been operating without them for a generation and had stopped noticing.

COINTELPRO, 1956 to 1971, destroyed the Black Panthers' formation work systematically and on purpose. The breakfast programs, the medical clinics, the political education classes — these were the most successful new-left formation infrastructure in America, and the federal government correctly identified them as more dangerous than rifles. The destruction was thorough. Nothing equivalent was rebuilt.

By the 1990s the kibbutzim were privatizing. By 2010 most had stopped being kibbutzim in any operative sense. The Catholic Worker movement persisted, and persists, but as a small witness, not a mass formation. The Wobblies are a romantic memory. The Communist Party USA is a fundraising apparatus. The unions that survive function as wage-bargaining without formation.

What remained was theory.

The Substitutes

A theory in search of people who can act on it has four candidate substitutes. The contemporary left has reached for all of them.

The rights frame. The civil rights movement, the women's movement, the gay rights movement, the trans rights movement — banked, in left memory, as left victories. They are not. They are liberal victories: the procedural-universal framework extended to new categories. Each new protected category is another atomized identity inside the apparatus. The architecture that dissolves substrate is reinforced, not opposed. The left has parasitized on the rights-extension train for sixty years, drawing emotional energy from victories that strengthen the very architecture the left's older self existed to oppose. The train is running out — not because moral energy is exhausted but because the procedural form has reached its limit. The train was never going where the left thought it was going.

Class war without the union hall. The Bernie campaigns, the DSA's growth from 6,000 to 90,000 members in seven years, Jacobin, the Mamdani win in New York. Real political events. But the population they activated has, almost without exception, never been inside the formation institutions that classical class politics required. The dues-paying DSA member who has never been in a union, never been in a parish, never been in a trade — she is mobilizing on the aesthetic of the union hall in a population that has never been in one. Shawn Fain at the UAW is doing actual class formation, on the ground, with real workers in real factories. He is the exception. He names the rule. The rest is class politics performed by graduates of the seminar room for an audience of graduates of the seminar room.

Identity politics. The most architecture-compatible answer the left could possibly produce. Identity is what an atomized population can have without being formed by anything. You don't need a tradition; you just need to be the identity. Fully legible, fully measurable, fully comparable, perfectly administrable. Which is why corporate America adopted it inside a decade. The apparatus loves identity politics because identity politics is a substrate-substitute that fits the apparatus's measurement requirements. It can be tracked, audited, reported, made into KPIs. The left believed this was a defeat for the apparatus. It was the apparatus's largest victory in a generation.

The cause. Palestine is the current name. Before it, Black Lives Matter. Before that, the antiwar movement, climate, the Sandinistas, anti-apartheid, Vietnam. Each functions as an omnicause: a unifying moral object large enough to organize a politics that cannot organize itself any other way. The keffiyeh is the kibbutz's ghost. The encampment is the parish's ghost. The teach-in is the union hall's ghost. The form is preserved, the substance is not. The cause produces solidarity-feeling without requiring the slow formation that produces actual solidarity. You can be in the movement by posting, by attending, by wearing the symbol — none of which is formation, all of which delivers the experience of belonging that formation used to deliver.

The four substitutes share a structure. None of them require formation. Each delivers, instead, an alternative to formation: rights-status, class-aesthetic, identity-being, moral cause. The substitutes are easier than formation. That is why they win. The left did not deliberately abandon formation. It was selected against it by the same architecture that selected the right's substrate.

The Field Test

There is one period in the recent record clean enough to read as a test.

Between 2014 and 2024 — roughly Ferguson to the second Trump election — American institutions undertook the largest formation-less mobilization in the history of the contemporary left. Antiracism, after George Floyd, became the default vocabulary of corporate America, the academy, philanthropy, federal hiring, K-12 curriculum, mainline Protestantism, and the cultural product of nearly every major studio. White Fragility sold three million copies. Land acknowledgments became standard. DEI training was mandatory at every Fortune 500. Every institution issued statements. Every institution had a chief diversity officer. Every institution made the commitments.

The decade ended in two years.

The collapse, when it came, was total. Not slow. Not contested. Total. By 2025 the DEI infrastructure was being dismantled at federal agencies, state universities, corporate boards, and most of the institutions that had built it. The land acknowledgments became embarrassing in retrospect. The training modules were quietly removed from intranets. The chief diversity officers were laid off.

What happened was not that the right won an argument. The right did not engage the argument. What happened is that there was nothing inside the building. The DEI architecture was ritual without formation. Statement-issuing without people who could be called to account on the basis of the statements. Compliance documents that produced no formed compliers. When the political wind reversed, there was no resistance, because resistance requires formed people, and the architecture had not formed anyone. It had only been administered.

The fuller diagnosis is darker. The architecture has not only dissolved the formative substrate — the parish, the union hall, the cell, the rule. It has dissolved the temporal substrate that formation requires: the capacity of a population to hold a frame, repeat a practice, attend to the same thing across years. Formation is repetition over time. The contemporary population's relationship to attention has been engineered into a refresh rate at which formation cannot occur. BLM lasted eighteen months at peak. Ukraine lasted six. Palestine has lasted longer because the underlying violence keeps refreshing the cycle, but the attention is event-driven, not formation-bearing. ICE protests spike and decay on news rhythm. Each frame is held by the population for as long as the algorithm holds it up, and not longer. The DEI architecture collapsed in two years not only because it was ritual without formation but because the population trying to use it had lost the capacity to sustain anything across the time formation requires. The substitutes don't just fail because they're not formation. Even as substitutes, they cannot find purchase, because the temporal ground beneath them has been paved.

This is the empirical record of formation-less mobilization on the left. It is short. It is precise. It is the cleanest test in the available data.

A regime, even a bad regime, takes longer than two years to dismantle. Schmitt's took twelve. Dugin's is operational and contested. Karp's is being built. The right's shortcut delivers something — a regime instrument, with the durability that machinery has. The left's shortcut does not deliver a regime. It delivers a vibe. The vibe collapses on first contact with anything harder.

This is why the left is hit worse by the formation collapse than the right. Karp's substitute at least produces something. Israel, Singapore, Switzerland — the security apparatus does form people, even if the cost is what the other essay named. The left's substitute produces nothing that survives the next election cycle. The right gets a regime. The left gets a feed.

The Last Door

There is one form of action the architecture leaves open to the formation-less individual.

Luigi Mangione, on a Manhattan sidewalk, December 4, 2024, shot Brian Thompson, the CEO of UnitedHealthcare, four times with a 3D-printed pistol. He was twenty-six. Penn engineering, valedictorian of his Baltimore prep school, Catholic-school formation thinned to nothing by the time he graduated, a Goodreads list rather than a reading group, a manifesto written for the algorithm rather than the cell. His politics were coherent. Health insurance was killing people. His back surgery had gone wrong. He had the diagnosis and he had the target and what he did not have was anywhere to put the politics that would have been action short of murder. The left infrastructure that could have absorbed him into something — a DSA chapter that meets weekly, a union local, a party cell, a tenants' association, even a reading group with discipline and accountability — does not exist on the form he could have entered. So the politics had nowhere to go except through the barrel.

The act is not the thing the essay needs to name. The response is. The TikToks. The wanted-poster posters. The McDonald's worker who turned him in being threatened across multiple states. The defenses on podcasts. The women writing him letters in jail. The crowd outside the courthouse. The response was not approval of murder. The response was recognition. The cheering crowd is the population that would have joined the cell if a cell had existed for them to join. They have his politics. They have his lack of formation. His act is the action their formation-less politics permits, and their cheering is the moment the post-substrate left sees itself in a mirror and does not look away.

The right has the same pattern, mirrored. Thomas Crooks at Butler in July 2024 — registered Republican, ActBlue donor, searches for both candidates, no party, no movement, no comrade, alone on a roof with a rifle. Ryan Routh at West Palm Beach two months later — a wandering Quixote with a Ukraine obsession and a list of grievances that did not cohere into a politics so much as accumulate into one. David DePape, the Pelosi attacker — hemp jewelry to nudism to Pizzagate to QAnon to MyPillow to Gamergate to JFK Jr. conspiracies, the entire algorithmic substitute-stack a single individual could ingest, ending in a hammer in an octogenarian's skull. None of them were formed. None of them were in militias in the older sense — the militia would have been a formation institution, slow, accountable, with rules and a chain of command. They were algorithmic individuals with grievance and access.

The structural prediction the architecture makes is now visible in the data. When formation collapses, mobilization becomes individual. The cell is unavailable. The party is unavailable as formation. The union, parish, guild, lodge, militia, all unavailable as formative institutions even where they persist as administrative shells. The political subject is alone with his politics. His politics are real. His isolation is real. The architecture that produced both is real. The action available to him at scale is individual action because individual is the only scale at which he exists.

The right's regime-shortcut and the left's cause-shortcut are elite responses to the formation collapse — Schmitt, Dugin, Karp, the DSA leadership, the encampment organizers, people with enough residual institutional purchase to imagine collective action. The lone wolf is the non-elite response. The formation collapse, for the median post-substrate individual with politics and no place to put them, does not produce a fascist movement or a socialist movement. It produces a young man alone in an apartment with a printed firearm and a feed.

Both wings now run a shadow politics of celebrated individual violence. On the right: Rittenhouse fully celebrated, Crooks briefly elevated, the J6 prisoners as constant reference, the attempts on Trump functioning as constitutive events in the regime narrative. On the left: Mangione as the flagship, the Israeli embassy shooter celebrated, the Tesla arsons cheered, the rumored attempts circulating every few weeks among populations the institutional left does not claim but functionally produces. The act becomes the only available legible political expression. The cell is illegible to the apparatus. The vibe is legible and produces nothing. The lone act is legible and produces something — a body, a fire, a news cycle, a political event the apparatus cannot ignore. The post-substrate political subject has discovered that he can be heard exactly once, by being arrested or killed.

The mistake to avoid is reading this as the threshold of a wave. The 1970s European pattern — RAF, Red Brigades, the long propagation through autonomous cells producing hundreds of acts a year — required two things the contemporary architecture has dissolved. Cells for absorption: the lone act was individual but the formation that produced the actor was collective, theoretical, slow, sustained. And a population capable of holding a frame across years, reading the same texts, recognizing each new act as continuous with the last. The first is gone for the same reason every cell-form is gone. The second is gone because attention itself has been engineered to a refresh rate that excludes the temporal structure waves require. The cheering for Mangione lasted two months. The encampments lasted six. The population that would have to propagate the wave cannot hold its own attention long enough to do so. The lone wolf is therefore the equilibrium output of the architecture, not the seed of a movement. He requires only what the architecture is specifically optimized to produce: short-cycle activation of an isolated individual, on his own time, with his own grievance, against his own target. The wolves continue. The wave does not arrive. The equilibrium itself is the diagnosis.

The cheering is the saddest thing in the essay. It is recognition without formation. It is the post-substrate population seeing in Mangione what it cannot become collectively — politically alive, willing to act on its convictions, unwilling to accept the substitute-stack as a substitute for a life. The cheering is correct in what it recognizes. The cheering is wrong in what it celebrates. The act is not the formation the cheering crowd actually wants. The act is what the absence of formation has left.

This is the condition the next section has to answer to.

The Rule

The traditions exist. The left has not always been formation-free, and the alternative to the lone wolf has been built before.

The Catholic Worker movement, founded 1933, still operates. Daily Mass, the works of mercy, voluntary poverty, hospitality houses, The Catholic Worker newspaper still printed at one cent a copy. Dorothy Day refused causes for which she could not pay personally. Peter Maurin wrote his Easy Essays in a verse form designed to be memorized and recited at the dinner table. They submitted their politics to a rule older than their politics. The rule was Catholicism.

Mondragón, founded 1956 in the Basque Country by Father José María Arizmendiarrieta, employs over 80,000 people in cooperative enterprises that include manufacturing, retail, finance, education, and research. The cooperatives are owned by the workers, governed by the workers, and reproduced through a school system that forms the workers before they enter the cooperatives. Mondragón has survived seventy years, weathered Franco, the EU, globalization, and the financial crisis. It is the most successful left-economic experiment of the twentieth century. It was founded by a priest. The formation precedes the cooperative; the cooperative does not produce the formation. The priest got the order right.

The base ecclesial communities of Latin America — comunidades eclesiales de base — formed in the 1960s and 1970s under liberation theology, combined Bible study, communal action, and Catholic discipline applied to political-economic critique. At their peak in the 1980s there were 80,000 to 100,000 of them in Brazil alone. They were the largest left-formation institution in the postwar Western Hemisphere. The Vatican strangled them under John Paul II and Ratzinger; what remains is diminished but real.

The Quakers — the Religious Society of Friends — have practiced consensus deliberation, witness, and silent worship for nearly four hundred years. They produced Lucretia Mott and the abolition movement, the early peace movement, prison reform, mental health reform. The formation comes from the meeting and the rule. The politics comes from the formation.

What these traditions share, across enormous theological differences, is the structure The Inheritance named in Benedict. Submission to a rule. A tradition older than the political program. Authority. Discipline. A formation that precedes the politics, that produces the kind of person who can do the politics. They are left traditions and they are formation-heavy and they work — measurably, on long timescales, against the architecture's pressure.

The contemporary left will not look at them. Cannot look at them. The reasons run deep. Therapeutic individualism forbids the submission a rule requires. Anti-clericalism forbids the religious authority every successful left-formation tradition has actually depended on. The progressive break with tradition forbids the recovery of older selves. Submit to a rule. The contemporary left cannot say this sentence.

So the resources are there and the door is locked from the inside.

The other essay closed on Benedict and the soil. This one has to close on something harder.

The right's shortcut produces a regime, and a regime is at least durable. The left's shortcut produces a vibe, and a vibe collapses on first contact. The right has the inheritance to burn through; the left has been at the end of its runway since 1989 and has been pretending otherwise for thirty-five years. The Benedictine answer is available, in left form, in traditions the contemporary left has identified as embarrassments and refused. The choice is to recover those traditions or to continue cycling through substitutes — rights, identity, class without union hall, the next omnicause — until the next Mangione. There will be more. The architecture is producing them faster than it is producing anything else.

There is no version of the cell that does not require the rule. There is no version of formation that does not require submission to something older than the political program. The left used to know this. The Wobblies knew it. Day knew it. Arizmendiarrieta knew it. The kibbutz founders knew it.

The choice is not the cause. The choice is between the rule and the lone wolf. The left, to be a left again, has to recover the capacity to submit to the first. Otherwise the second is the politics it has, whether it claims him or not.

Or the substrate stays gone. And the cause keeps coming. And the vibe keeps collapsing. And the next election the left wins, if it wins one, will be won on the apparatus's terms, against an opponent whose substrate-substitute is more durable, and unable to maintain itself beyond a single news cycle. And the lone wolves keep walking through the last door because the architecture has closed every other door.

The kite called the fall autonomy. The cause calls it solidarity. The act calls it justice. Different names. Same fall.
