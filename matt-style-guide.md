# Matt's Essay Style Guide

## Sentence Structure

**Break compound sentences.** If a sentence has a comma splice or a conjunction joining two independent clauses, make it two sentences. Period instead of comma. Period instead of semicolon. Period instead of em dash when both sides can stand alone.

- Bad: "This is a mistake, not because prophecy is true but because it does not matter whether prophecy is true."
- Good: "This is a mistake. Not because prophecy is true but because it does not matter whether prophecy is true."

**Trust the short sentence.** A sentence can be three words. It can be a fragment. If the thought is complete, stop.

- "Now that project lies in ruins."
- "Or so it appears."
- "It removes the competitor."

**No trailing qualifications.** End sentences on the strong word. Don't soften the landing with additional clauses, caveats, or restatements.

- Bad: "It is the most rational objective, and the one most achievable by air power alone."
- Good: "It is the most rational objective."


## Transitions and Connectives

**Cut leading conjunctions when the paragraph break already signals the turn.** "And" and "But" at the start of sentences are often doing work the structure already does. Remove them unless the rhythm specifically needs the softening.

- Bad: "And Netanyahu's coalition wants proxy silence."
- Good: "Finally, Netanyahu's coalition wants proxy silence."
- Bad: "And yet there is a single outcome..."
- Good: "Yet there is a single outcome..."

**Cut throat-clearing.** Phrases like "here is the key," "here is what matters," "it is worth noting that" — delete. Just say the thing.

- Bad: "And here is Girard's uncomfortable insight: *it works*."
- Good: "Girard's uncomfortable insight: *it works*."

**No narrative stage directions.** Don't tell the reader you're about to make a point. Don't announce what the next section will do. Don't say "this is not speculation" unless the reader would genuinely think it was.


## Word Choice

**Strip qualifiers.** Remove: actually, in fact, literally, implicitly, essentially, arguably, conveniently, quite, rather, somewhat, fairly, primarily. If the qualifier is doing real work, find a stronger noun or verb instead.

**Strip intensifiers.** Remove: very, extremely, remarkably, extraordinarily, incredibly. The sentence should carry its own weight.

**Prefer the plain word.** "Exhausted, indebted, fractured" not "exhausted, indebted, politically fractured." The adjective before "fractured" is padding. The reader knows you mean politically.

**Cut redundant adjectives.** "The primary competitor" becomes "the competitor" when context makes clear there's only one that matters.


## Paragraph and Section Level

**One idea per paragraph.** If a paragraph makes its point in three sentences, it doesn't need five. Don't restate the conclusion in different words.

**Don't repeat the framework.** Once you've explained a concept (e.g., the scapegoat mechanism, mimetic desire), trust the reader to hold it. Don't re-explain it each time you invoke it.

**Let lists do their work.** When a paragraph builds to a list ("The neocon gets Japan 1945. Trump gets his photo op..."), the list *is* the argument. Don't add a summary sentence after it ("Everyone wins") — that's saying the same thing a third time.

**Paragraph breaks are punctuation.** A one-sentence paragraph is a legitimate rhetorical move. Use it for emphasis. "They build temples." "Now that project lies in ruins." These land harder alone.


## Tone and Register

**Declarative, not exploratory.** The essay voice states. It does not wonder, speculate, hedge, or muse. When uncertain, frame the uncertainty as a statement: "There is no precedent because the thing that is happening is unprecedented."

**No self-congratulation.** Don't signal that an insight is clever. "So the convergence exists. It is not just theoretically elegant" — cut the first sentence. Start with the claim about elegance.

**Earn the prophetic register.** The essay's closing sections use repetition and incantation ("And the long peace will be real. And the Temple may be built."). This register is permitted only after extensive analytical work. It must be earned, not deployed early.

**Methodological asides in plain language.** When addressing the reader directly ("A note on method..."), be maximally clear. Short sentences. No jargon. This is where you build trust with the skeptic.


## What Not to Cut

**Concrete specifics.** Names, dates, body counts, historical examples — these earn trust. Never cut them for brevity.

**The unexpected pivot.** When a section turns ("Or so it appears"), give it room. The turn is doing structural work.

**Rhythm in closing passages.** Anaphora, repetition, and long cumulative sentences are earned moves in final sections. Don't tighten these the way you tighten analytical prose.
