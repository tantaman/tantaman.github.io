# The Wheel

The cage is visible. The next question is escape.

Every obvious exit has been tried. Every escape route runs through the same engine that built the cage. This chapter forecloses them all. Not out of nihilism. Out of honesty. The reader who has not felt the impossibility of escape through effort will not be ready for what comes after.

## The Physicians of Decay

The poststructuralists were right about power. They were wrong about what to do with rightness.

Nietzsche wanted revaluation. He wanted the creation of new values from the rubble of the old. He wanted the *Ubermensch* -- not a superman but a human being capable of creating meaning without metaphysical crutches. He got professors. He got tenure committees adjudicating the correct interpretation of the will to power. He got an entire academic industry devoted to the critique of all values that cannot itself produce a single value.

Derrida showed that every text deconstructs itself -- that binary oppositions (nature/culture, speech/writing, presence/absence) contain their own undoing. The insight was genuine. The application was catastrophic. If every hierarchy of meaning deconstructs, then no meaning is stable. If no meaning is stable, then the self has nothing to stand on. The deconstructed self is not liberated. It is dissolved. And the dissolved self is the ideal consumer: no transcendent anchor means total availability for market capture.

Foucault traced how power produces knowledge, how institutions create the subjects they claim merely to govern, how the very categories through which we understand ourselves are technologies of control. Every word of it was correct. The analysis was perfect. The analysis produced nothing. It could not produce anything because the tools of production had been dissolved by the analysis.

Lyotard declared the end of grand narratives. Baudrillard declared the end of reality itself -- everything is simulation, copies without originals, signs referring to nothing. Each diagnosis was astute. Each left the patient worse. They were physicians of decay who described the disease with increasing precision while the patient died on the table.

The poststructuralists became what they critiqued. They meant to dissolve oppressive structures. They dissolved *all* structures. The oppressive ones were replaced by the market. The liberating ones were not replaced at all. The net effect was to clear the field for capital, which needs no structures, no traditions, no transcendent claims -- only liquid selves available for deployment.

## Critique Was Captured

Marx's analysis of exploitation was correct. The extraction of surplus value from labor by capital is a real mechanism. The tendency of capital to concentrate is a real tendency. The function of ideology as the ideas of the ruling class is a real function.

A new ruling class now uses Marx's critique as its own instrument of domination.

The professional-managerial credentialed class controls the universities, the media, the NGOs, the cultural institutions, and the administrative state. This class speaks the language of liberation. It critiques every form of authority except its own. It demolishes every justification for power except the credentialed class's. The selectivity proves it is class interest, not liberation.

The credential functions as monopolistic guild gatekeeping disguised as expertise. The lawyer, the doctor, the professor, the licensed therapist -- each requires a credential that takes years and tens of thousands of dollars to acquire. The credential does not certify competence. It certifies membership. It restricts supply. It maintains the class boundary.

Marx would recognize this instantly. A class that controls the means of cultural production, that reproduces itself through credentialing institutions it controls, that justifies its position through an ideology it generates and enforces -- this is a ruling class by any Marxian definition. That it uses Marxist language to describe its own position as oppressed is the final irony. The critique was captured by the class it was designed to critique.

## Will to Power Beneath All Systems

Socialism attributes domination exclusively to capitalism. The claim is that hierarchy, exploitation, and oppression are products of a specific economic system. Change the system and the domination disappears.

The historical record refutes this with brutal consistency.

Indigenous civilizations practiced slavery. The Aztecs sacrificed captives on an industrial scale. West African kingdoms built economies on the slave trade before Europeans arrived. Polynesian societies maintained rigid hierarchical castes. The Comanche empire was built on raiding, enslaving, and territorial conquest.

These are not embarrassing exceptions. They are the human baseline. The will to dominate is not a product of capitalism. It is a feature of the species. Capitalism's true "offense" -- if offense is the right word -- is making domination transparent. Contracts and ledgers make the extraction visible. Pre-capitalist societies mystified domination through theology, kinship obligation, and sacred hierarchy. Capitalism stripped the mystification. The extraction continued.

The question is not whether hierarchy exists. It has existed in every human society with surplus. The question is what forms of hierarchy constrain the will to power toward creation rather than destruction. This question cannot be asked from the socialist framework because the socialist framework insists that the problem is the system, not the species.

## Mimetic Desire

Rene Girard identified the engine beneath the engine.

We do not know what we want. We learn what to want by imitating others. Desire is not spontaneous. It is mimetic -- borrowed from models whose desires we copy. The child wants the toy because the other child wants it. The consumer wants the product because the advertisement shows someone desirable wanting it. The intellectual wants the position because a rival occupies it.

When the model is distant -- a celebrity, a historical figure, a saint -- the imitation produces aspiration. You want what they have and the distance protects you from rivalry. When the model is close -- a colleague, a friend, a sibling -- the imitation produces rivalry. You both want the same thing. Neither can have it without the other losing it. The rivals become doubles. They mirror each other with increasing precision while insisting on their radical difference.

Girard called this the monstrous double. The rivals converge structurally while diverging rhetorically. Each defines himself against the other. Each becomes more like the other in the act of opposing. The Cold War was the paradigmatic case: two superpowers whose structures converged (industrial economies, nuclear arsenals, surveillance states, imperial ambitions) while their rhetoric maximized difference (capitalism vs. communism, freedom vs. equality).

The revolutionary becomes the tyrant through the same mechanism. Not through corruption. Through mimesis. The revolutionary models himself on the tyrant -- studies him, opposes him, mirrors him -- until the opposition produces a structural copy. The revolution succeeds. The copy sits in the tyrant's chair. The logic is not moral. It is mechanical.

Contemporary politics runs on mimetic escalation. Each side defines itself against the other. Each becomes more like the other in the act of opposing. The progressive who obsessively monitors conservative speech and the conservative who obsessively monitors progressive speech are mimetic doubles. The monitoring intensifies the mirroring. The mirroring intensifies the monitoring. Girard, drawing on Clausewitz, called it "escalation to extremes."

Opposition intensifies what it opposes. This is the wheel. You push against the engine and your pushing turns it.

## Institutions Convert

Every institution built to help people eventually converts to serve its own survival.

The mechanism is structural, not moral. An institution that does not prioritize its survival does not survive. The institutions that remain are the ones that converted. Selection operates on institutions the same way it operates on organisms: the ones that persist are the ones that prioritized persistence.

The hospital that began as a place of care becomes a billing system with a medical department attached. The university that began as a place of formation becomes a credentialing factory with a library attached. The church that began as a community of encounter becomes a brand with a congregation attached. The nonprofit that began as a mission becomes a fundraising operation with a program attached.

Tools created by one political faction are inherited by opponents and weaponized. The surveillance state built by liberals is inherited by authoritarians. The campus speech codes designed by progressives are deployed against progressives. The executive powers expanded by one president are wielded by the next. Both sides fail to recognize that the tools themselves are the problem. The institution converts whoever holds it.

Outsourcing all care to the state atrophies the community bonds and mutual aid capacity that remain civilization's only insurance against systemic collapse. When the state provides, the community stops providing. When the community stops providing, the capacity to provide atrophies. When the capacity atrophies, the state's provision becomes indispensable. The dependency deepens. The community dissolves further. The state grows.

## The Engine Runs on Will

The machine eats the world because it is fueled by the will to help.

Abstract compassion -- compassion for categories rather than persons -- allows you to feel virtuous without encountering actual suffering. You donate to the organization. You sign the petition. You post the hashtag. Each act produces the sensation of moral engagement without the friction of genuine encounter. The sensation is indistinguishable from the real thing. It is not the real thing.

The machine converts good intentions into institutional power with perfect efficiency. Every dollar donated, every petition signed, every awareness raised enters the machine as moral energy and exits as administrative apparatus. The apparatus employs people whose livelihoods depend on the problem's continuation. The incentive to solve the problem is weaker than the incentive to perpetuate it. The machine grows. The problem persists. The donors feel virtuous.

This is not cynicism about compassion. It is a structural observation about what happens when compassion is mediated by institutions that must survive. The compassion is real. The mediation corrupts it. The corruption is not a malfunction. It is the machine working as machines work.

## The Ratchet Beneath the Ratchet

Return to the gravity theory. Apply it to reform itself.

Surplus accumulates. Complexity compounds. Power concentrates. No agent stands outside the gravitational field. The redistributor accumulates power. The regulator becomes a power center. The revolutionary becomes the new elite. Even the societies that tried hardest to prevent accumulation -- the immediate-return foragers who demanded sharing and ridiculed the boastful -- had the ratchet running through reputation and obligation.

If the ratchet operates beneath every system -- capitalist, socialist, anarchist, communal -- then changing the system does not stop the ratchet. It changes the medium through which the ratchet operates. The ratchet in a capitalist society operates through capital accumulation. The ratchet in a socialist society operates through bureaucratic accumulation. The ratchet in a religious society operates through spiritual authority accumulation. The mechanism is the same. The currency differs.

This is the wheel. Every attempt to stop it through effort feeds it. Every resistance generates a new form of power. Every revolution produces a new elite. Every critique becomes a new orthodoxy. The wheel runs on will. More will turns it faster.

## Principalities and Powers

Paul of Tarsus, writing to the church at Ephesus, named the enemy: "For our struggle is not against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the powers of this dark world and against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly realms."

This is not supernatural demonology. It is structural diagnosis. The "principalities and powers" are self-perpetuating logics -- patterns that shape souls through behavioral compliance, that outlive any individual, that operate through institutions without requiring any individual's conscious cooperation.

Rome understood power at this level. Rome's genius was not military. It was formational. Rome colonized populations through infrastructure, law, and the imperial cult. The roads were not gifts. They were control mechanisms. The law was not justice. It was standardization. The imperial cult -- the requirement to burn incense to the emperor -- was not theological. It was a loyalty test that, repeated over years, became genuine submission. Compliance became formation. The conquered became Romans.

Modern principalities operate identically. The platform that shapes your attention. The therapy framework that shapes your self-understanding. The market logic that shapes your sense of worth. Each is a principality -- a self-perpetuating pattern that outlives any individual, operates through institutions, and shapes souls through behavioral compliance.

The enemy is not people. It is patterns. Counter-formation through alternative communities, not institutional reform, is the only structural response. You cannot defeat a pattern by reforming the institution that carries it. You can only build an alternative pattern and inhabit it.

## The Foreclosure

The thesis: naive faith was flawed but generative. It produced communities, practices, structures that sustained human life. It was captured and corrupted -- but it was alive.

The antithesis: critical dissolution was correct but catastrophic. The critique of power was accurate. The dissolution of inherited structures was justified. The result was the cage. The deconstructed self, the liquid self, the self available for capture. The critique dissolved the disease and the immune system simultaneously.

The synthesis cannot come from more critique. It cannot come from more willing. It cannot come from a louder voice, a better theory, a more rigorous analysis. The analysis is complete. The cage is named. The wheel is visible.

Something outside the system is required. The system has no category for "outside." The void was always a construction. Meaninglessness is a choice that has been tested and found wanting. But the alternative cannot be willed into existence. Will is the fuel the engine runs on. More fuel, faster engine.

The analysis has reached its limit. It points at a door it cannot open.

*The first man tries harder. More research. More contingency plans. More attempts to control the outcome. Each attempt makes things worse. His wife withdraws from his management. He picks up his youngest from daycare and on the drive home the boy says "Daddy sing" and he opens his mouth and what comes out is thin and off-key and he realizes he hasn't sung in months.*

*He used to sing constantly. In the car, in the shower, while cooking. Somewhere in the planning, the music stopped.*

*The second man does something surprising. He stops trying to fix it. He signs up for a woodworking class on Tuesday nights. He has no particular aptitude. The appeal is in the material -- the resistance of the grain, the requirement of attention. You cannot worry about November while operating a table saw.*

*He comes home smelling like sawdust and his boys climb on him and his wife laughs at the shavings in his hair. He's building a crib. It will not be beautiful. It will hold a baby.*

*The first man's fear has metastasized into insomnia, distance, a spreadsheet, a secret. The second man's fear remains itself. It has nowhere to metastasize. He felt it, named it, shared it. It remains fear. It does not become a cage.*
