 Where V2 is clearly better:                                           
                                                                        
  1. Structural logic. V1's diagnosis is: know thyself → God's          
  persistence → pathologies → analysis of power → physicians of decay.  
  The ordering feels somewhat arbitrary — why does "God's persistence"  
  come before pathologies? Why is "physicians of decay" separate from   
  "the wheel"? V2's ordering (name the self → genealogy → why this      
  direction → the cage → the wheel) has genuine logical necessity. Each 
  chapter earns the next.                                               
  2. The Cage consolidation. V1 scattered the cage material across three
   chapters — Ch3 (pathologies), Ch5 (physicians of decay), and Ch12
  (three inversions + generational wheel). V2 puts all of it in one     
  chapter with the "theological demands without their resolution"       
  framing, which is stronger than anything V1 had. Crucifixion without  
  Easter, original sin without absolution, Calvinist labor without      
  Calvinist promise — this directly ties the pathologies to the         
  genealogy in a way V1 never did.                                      
  3. The gravity theory gets its own chapter (ch4). In V1, the          
  thermodynamic ratchet was crammed into The Wheel alongside Girard. V2
  separates why the trajectory bent this way (gravity, amplifiers,
  legibility, two filters) from why every exit fails (mimetic desire,
  will as fuel). Cleaner.
  4. Foucault in the right place. V1 had the confessing-animal insight
  buried in ch4's analysis of power. V2 puts it in ch3 as a genealogical
   move — tracing how pastoral power migrated into therapy. More logical
   and more powerful there.
  5. Less repetition. V1 repeated the self-management capstone in
  multiple places, revisited the three pathologies more than once, and
  had Ch12 doing work that should have been done earlier. V2 is tighter.

  Where V1 is better:

  1. The opening line. V1: "You have a self. You did not build it." V2:
  "Everyone thinks they know what it means to know yourself." V1's is a
  thesis. V2's is a setup. V1's is better.
  2. Chapter length and pace. V1 chapters average ~3,000 words. V2's
  diagnosis chapters are longer — Ch5 (The Cage) is probably 3,500+. V1
  moved faster. The book should feel like it's pulling you forward, not
  asking you to settle in.
  3. The preface. V1 has an excellent one. V2 doesn't have one yet. The
  preface does crucial work — it tells you the parallel story is
  autobiographical, it explains the method, it earns trust.
  4. Voice consistency. V1 feels more like one person talking. V2,
  despite our rewrite, still has moments where the essay origins show —
  particularly in Ch5 where absorbed material from different sources
  creates subtle tonal shifts.
  5. V1's later chapters. Ch9 (Mysticism, ~4,283 words) and Ch10 (What
  Counts as Knowing, ~5,828 words) are reportedly the book's strongest
  material — the philosophical core. V2 hasn't written these yet, so the
   comparison is incomplete. But V1's architecture gave these chapters
  more room (Movement III was 4 chapters) while V2's plan gives them
  less (4 chapters for mysticism through arrival, same count but with
  the Rupture restored).

  Genuine concerns about V2:

  1. Ch2 is too thin. At ~1,800 words it's half the length of its
  neighbors. After cutting the material that moved to Ch5, what remains
  (authenticity + grammar + outputs) might not carry a full chapter.
  Consider whether Ch2 and Ch3 should be merged, or whether Ch2 needs
  more development — perhaps the employable subject material or the
  "exhaustion that cannot rest" could stay here in compressed form.
  2. The Nine Months interludes. They were written for V1's chapter
  progression. The V2 rewrite kept them mostly intact, but do they still
   track? The first man's arc (research → spreadsheets → isolation →
  wife's confrontation) needs to match the argument's arc, not just
  appear at chapter breaks.
  3. Movement II transition. In V1, The Wheel (Ch6) ended Movement I
  with the gravity/grace distinction from Weil, and Ch7 (Honest Record)
  opened Movement II. In V2, The Wheel (Ch6) ends Movement I with
  "grace" and Ch7 opens what might be Movement II — but the movement
  boundary isn't marked. The shift from "everything is captured" to "but
   this record resists capture" is the book's most important turn. It
  needs to land.

  Overall verdict: V2's architecture is superior. The logical ordering,
  the consolidation, the Foucault placement, the theological-demands
  framing — all improvements. But V2 needs V1's pace and voice. The
  chapters should be shorter and faster. The book should feel like
  descent — each chapter pulling you deeper — not like a comprehensive
  treatment of topics.

  The strongest version would keep V2's structure with V1's pace and
  opening.
