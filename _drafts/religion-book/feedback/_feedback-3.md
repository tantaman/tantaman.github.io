1. Ch. 5's load-bearing fences passage is the book's biggest unforced 
  error. Your own feedback file flags it (line 72-81). "Men and women   
  are different, and the differences matter for how children are raised"
   and "Sexuality channeled produces civilization; unchanneled, it      
  produces what we now observe" — these are enormous claims slipped in
  as asides in a parenthetical. The book has spent four chapters earning
   the reader's trust through rigorous, carefully argued analysis. This
  passage spends that trust in two sentences that assert without
  arguing. The feedback is right: either give them the full treatment
  they require or cut them. As it stands, they'll be used to dismiss the
   entire book by exactly the readers the book is trying to reach.

  2. Buddhism is the book's strongest unengaged competitor. The book
  claims the biblical record uniquely outperforms all alternatives, and
  Ch. 7 does serious comparative work against Chinese historiography,
  Greek tragedy, and the Mahabharata. But Buddhism — which has its own
  analysis of the wheel (samsara), its own millennial institutional
  history, its own contemplative tradition converging with Eckhart
  (which Ch. 9 uses), and its own internal critique tradition — gets one
   respectful paragraph in Ch. 3 and then is filed under "sophisticated
  shrug when secularized." The same is true of Christianity when
  secularized, and the book gives Christianity 13 chapters. A reader
  with any depth in Buddhist philosophy will notice this asymmetry. The
  book doesn't need to refute Buddhism — but it needs to engage it
  seriously enough that the dismissal doesn't look like the selective
  critique Ch. 5 diagnoses in the credentialed class.

  3. The catchphrases are becoming the book's own grammar. Your feedback
   file catches this (line 46-61) and the irony is sharp: the argument
  about how grammar forecloses thought is developing its own grammar.
  "The engine cannot metabolize." "The wheel turns regardless." "Every
  liberation becomes what it fought." "The grasping self." These appear
  3-4 times each across the manuscript. Individually effective.
  Cumulatively they start doing the reader's thinking for her — tokens
  she nods at rather than propositions she wrestles with. An editorial
  pass that keeps the strongest occurrence of each and varies or cuts
  the rest would tighten the second half significantly.

  4. Small reference error in Ch. 10's opening. Line 5: "The chapter
  before last forced it into the open." The ground encounter happens in
  Ch. 9 (Mysticism Pulls) — the previous chapter, not "before last."
  Looks like a leftover from a different chapter ordering.

  5. The Deepened Encounter is partially resolved but still implicit.
  The _arc.md and _feedback-2.md both flag that the honest record's
  transformation from "structural critique" (Ch. 7) to "grounding
  narrative" never gets a dedicated moment. Our Ch. 10 edits help
  significantly — the new material argues that the record earns
  authority through testable criteria (independence of witnesses,
  predictions scored by reality). But the experiential moment — where
  the record is received as ground rather than evaluated as evidence —
  is still implicit. The _feedback-2.md recommendation to weave this
  into Ch. 11's opening (before the eschatology) still has merit: after
  the epistemological reckoning of Ch. 10, the record can be received
  differently, and that reception is what makes the rupture material
  land.

  Of these, #1 is the most urgent because it's a vulnerability that
  costs the book readers for free. #2 is the most structurally important
   because it's a gap in the comparative argument. #3 is editorial work
  that would sharpen the whole manuscript. #4 is a two-second fix. #5 is
   the subtlest and might resolve itself if the reader is tracking.
