# Chapter 6: The Wheel

Movement I has been building a case. You are inside a cage you cannot see, assembled by forces you did not choose, producing a self that generates its own suffering while experiencing that suffering as freedom. The genealogy traced the path. The gravity theory explained why this path won. The cage chapter made the abstract personal.

Now comes the purpose of the entire first movement: to foreclose all exits through effort.

This is not cruelty. It is honesty. Every exit you can think of — critique, revolution, reform, therapy, self-improvement, political action — has been tried. Every one has been captured. This chapter shows why. Not to produce despair but to clear the ground. Because until the exits through effort have been honestly foreclosed, you will keep trying them. And each attempt will feed the engine you are trying to escape.

## Critique Was Captured

The poststructuralists were the most brilliant diagnosticians of power the modern world has produced. Foucault showed that power is not merely repressive but *productive* — it creates the very subjects who then exercise and transmit it. The prison does not merely confine criminals; it *produces* the category "criminal." The confession does not merely extract truth; it *produces* the "interior" that is then available for extraction. Derrida showed that every text undermines itself — that every system of meaning depends on exclusions it cannot acknowledge.

These were genuine discoveries. They mapped the cage with unprecedented precision.

And then they were captured.

The critical tools were absorbed by the credentialed class — the professional-managerial elite that controls universities, media, nonprofits, and the administrative apparatus of the state. Critique became credential. The ability to *deconstruct* became a class marker indistinguishable in function from the old aristocratic markers it claimed to dismantle.

The revolutionary insight — that power operates through discourse, that categories are constructed, that knowledge serves interests — was converted into an institutional program. Diversity, equity, and inclusion. Unconscious bias training. The critical examination of privilege. All administered by the very institutional apparatus the critique was supposed to expose.

This is not a betrayal of the critique. It is the critique's *inherent logic*. Every critique that succeeds becomes an institution. Every institution requires administrators. Every administrator has interests. The critique of power becomes a *form* of power — wielded by people who sincerely believe they are dismantling the thing they are reproducing.

Alongside the capture of critique, a particular epistemology was installed that forecloses all rational escape. Standpoint epistemology holds that all knowledge is socially situated — that where you stand determines what you can see. This contains a genuine insight. But if all knowledge is socially situated, then standpoint epistemology itself is merely situated. It cannot claim universal authority because it has denied the possibility of universal authority. It is a perspective arguing that all we have are perspectives.

The political consequence is devastating. If you cannot argue someone into understanding — if they either occupy a standpoint that grants access or they don't — then persuasion is impossible. The only remaining move is institutional capture. This explains the simultaneous dominance and impotence of the progressive left: optimized for hiring committees and editorial boards, unable to win elections that require voter persuasion.

The physicians diagnosed the disease. Then they opened a practice. The practice requires patients. The supply of patients must never run out. The disease must never be cured.

## Mimetic Desire

If the capture of critique explains how exits are sealed, Girard explains *why* they keep reopening and resealing in the same pattern.

René Girard's insight: human desire is not autonomous. It is *triangular*. Subject, object, mediator. We do not want things because they are intrinsically desirable. We want them because *someone else* wants them. The child does not want the toy until another child reaches for it. The adult does not want the career, the partner, the house, until they see it wanted by those they admire, compete with, wish to resemble.

We are mimetic creatures. We borrow our desires from one another like flames passed from candle to candle.

This produces rivalry. The model of desire becomes the rival for its object. The closer the rivals, the more intense the conflict. And here is Girard's most counterintuitive insight: the most intense conflicts occur between the most *similar* parties. As rivalry intensifies, rivals become more alike while convinced of absolute opposition.

Revolutionary Marxism replicated Christianity with structural precision. The proletariat replaces the chosen people. The revolution replaces the eschaton. The party replaces the church. The vanguard replaces the clergy. An ideology that defined itself through opposition to Christianity could not escape Christianity's gravitational pull. The more intensely it struggled against its predecessor, the more completely it absorbed that predecessor's structure.

This is the monstrous double. The rival becomes the mirror. The opposition becomes the reproduction. The revolution becomes the ancien regime in new costume.

And without a transcendent reference — without something that breaks the mimetic cycle from outside — the mechanism runs forever. Subject imitates model. Model becomes rival. Rivalry intensifies. Crisis erupts. The community converges its violence on a single victim — the scapegoat. The expulsion temporarily restores peace. The peace holds until the next crisis.

This is the hidden structure beneath your politics, your office dynamics, your social media frenzies, your family Thanksgivings. It operates in every human community ever documented. It cannot be eliminated by will. It can only be interrupted from outside.

## Principalities and Powers

Paul told the early church: "We wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world."

We moderns read this and think: superstition. The ancients believed in demons. We have Foucault and systems theory.

But what if Paul already knew what Foucault discovered?

The Greek terms repay attention. *Archai* — principalities, but also origins, first principles, the foundational logic of a thing. *Exousiai* — authorities, the capacity to act that inheres in structures. *Kosmokratoras* — world-rulers, those who administer the age.

These are not demons with pitchforks. These are the *spirits of systems*. The inner logic of empires. The self-perpetuating nature of institutions. The way a structure shapes everyone inside it regardless of individual intention.

Paul is telling the early church: your enemy is not Nero. Kill Nero and another Nero appears. Your enemy is not the centurion or the tax collector. These are flesh and blood, and flesh and blood is not your war. Your war is against the *principality* that produces endless Neros. Against the *power* that makes tax collection automatic and normalized. Against the *world-rulers* who administer the age so completely that their rule seems like nature itself.

Every institution built to help becomes a system of control. The trajectory is always the same: help leads to verification, verification leads to monitoring, monitoring leads to discipline, discipline leads to control. The food stamp program begins as generosity and becomes a surveillance apparatus. The school begins as education and becomes a sorting machine. The machine lets its builders care about humanity in the abstract while remaining indifferent to humans in the particular. You can vote for programs that help "the poor" without ever sitting with a poor person.

And every tool you build will be seized. The surveillance apparatus designed to catch terrorists will be turned on dissidents. The hate speech laws designed to protect the vulnerable will silence the inconvenient. The centralized power assembled for progressive purposes will be wielded by the next administration for regressive ones.

The principalities colonize the interior through the exterior. They do not need to argue. They only need to mandate behavior. The argument happens automatically, inside the soul of the compliant, who must reconcile their actions with their self-image.

We have forgotten how to fight principalities. We argue. We critique. We post. We vote. All of this is flesh-and-blood resistance to a power that is not flesh and blood. It is fighting the centurion while ignoring the empire.

## The Engine Runs on Will

And here is the terrible synthesis that Movement I has been building toward.

The engine — the entire system, from the cage to the wheel to the fire within — runs on will. Every act of will feeds it. Liberation movements are metabolized into institutional power. Revolutionary energy is converted into administrative apparatus. Even the will to *stop* — the deliberate decision to rest, to resist, to refuse — is captured and converted. "Self-care" becomes a product. "Mindfulness" becomes a corporate program. "Resistance" becomes a brand.

The engine does not care about the content of your will. Liberation, justice, holiness, equality — it metabolizes all of them. It does not matter *what* you will. It matters *that* you will. The willing itself is the fuel.

Stoicism tries to stop the engine by withdrawing desire. But withdrawal is itself a program — a discipline, a practice, a project of the self. The Stoic who masters detachment has produced a *self that is detached*, which is still a production, still fuel.

Buddhism tries to stop the engine by dissolving the self. But the dissolution is itself an act of will — "I will dissolve the self" — and the will reasserts itself in the act of dissolution.

Poststructuralism tries to stop the engine by deconstructing all stable grounds. But deconstruction is itself a methodology — rigorous, productive, endlessly generative of new papers, new positions, new tenure files. The critique of power becomes the most powerful game in the academy.

Socialism tries to stop the engine by changing the economic system. But the will to power precedes capitalism. Potlatch was domination through display. Roman slavery was domination through conquest. Medieval serfdom was domination through land monopoly. The Chinese imperial examination system was domination through credentialing — the identical mechanism that operates today, fifteen centuries later. The deepest dishonesty is to locate the source of domination in an institution rather than in humans themselves. Capitalism didn't teach man to exploit. Capitalism taught exploitation to keep receipts.

The socialist revolution, wherever tried, has reproduced the hierarchy it overthrew — not because the revolutionaries were hypocrites but because the amplifiers persist. The revolutionary party is itself an amplifier. The committee that seizes the state is itself a power center. The administrators of the new order discover, with genuine surprise, that they enjoy administering. The wheel turns.

Each attempt to escape the engine *through effort* is fuel. The harder you try to stop, the faster the wheel turns. The more brilliantly you diagnose, the more thoroughly you are captured. Knowledge of the cage does not free you from the cage. Naming the principality does not defeat the principality. Understanding the mechanism does not disable the mechanism.

This is not a riddle with a clever answer. It is the honest diagnosis.

## What Is Required

If will is the engine — if every act of will feeds the system you are trying to escape — then something *outside the system* is required.

The modern self is a cage, assembled by historical forces, producing suffering while appearing as freedom. Every attempt to escape the cage through will and critique is captured by the very system it attempts to escape. Something that operates by a different logic — something that does not run on will — is required.

Not another program. Not another critique. Not another revolution. Something that the engine cannot metabolize because it is not fuel. Something that the principality cannot capture because it is not flesh and blood. Something that the will cannot grasp because it is not a product of will.

The secular analysis has reached its ceiling. It can diagnose. It cannot step outside. The most honest conclusion a purely secular investigation can reach is this: there is no outside.

Unless something operates by a different logic entirely.

The rest of this book is an attempt to say what that something might be. But it can only be said after the exits through effort have been honestly foreclosed — after you have seen, clearly, that the cage cannot be thought away, reformed away, critiqued away, or willed away.

The cage is real. The wheel turns. And no act of will can stop them.

But there is something that does not run on will. There has always been something that does not run on will. And it has been waiting, patiently, beneath everything the will has built — beneath the cage, beneath the wheel, beneath the fire — for the moment when the will exhausts itself and stops.

That moment is called grace.

We are not there yet. But the ground has been prepared.

*The first man tries harder. Each attempt makes things worse. He closes the spreadsheet and then reopens it. He comes home early and then disappears into his phone. He tells his wife he'll be more present and then forgets what his youngest said at dinner because his mind was calculating hospital distances.*

*His wife says: You've been planning my funeral for seven months.*

*He says: I've been planning for what happens if —*

*She says: That's the same thing.*

*And it is. He knows it is. The spreadsheet is a coffin made of cells and formulas, and he's been sleeping next to her every night inside it.*

*The second man stops trying to fix it. He is afraid and he says so. He calls his buddy, the one whose wife also had a rough delivery. They drink beer on the buddy's porch. His buddy says, "How are you actually doing?" He says, "Scared." His buddy says, "Yeah." They drink their beer.*

*Nothing is solved. Everything is different.*
