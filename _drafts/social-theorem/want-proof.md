# The Oldest Want: A Generalized Proof

*This proof extends the original Want proof to cover all subject positions.
It adds a Strategy Theorem showing that every personality type fails for the same structural reason,
and closes on the shape of the exit — without yet naming it.*

---

## Definitions

*(D1–D8 carried forward from the original proof.)*

**D1. Subject** — A locus of experience that is not exhausted by any finite description of it. Denote the full subject *S*.

**D2. Form** — Any finite, transmissible representation of *S*. Denote a form *F(S)*, where *F* is a lossy projection operator. By definition, *F(S) ⊂ S*.

**D3. Remainder** — The portion of the subject not captured by a given form. Denote *R = S \ F(S)*. By D2, *R ≠ ∅*.

**D4. Attention** — A finite receptive capacity belonging to an embodied agent. Attention *A* operates through a field Φ_B constituted by a grammar — a formed structure of categories, concepts, and expectations. Attention cannot receive what the grammar cannot parse.

**D5. Legibility** — The property of being parseable by another's attentional grammar. *S* is legible to agent *B* if and only if *S* has taken a form *F(S)* that *B*'s grammar can receive.

**D6. Recognition** — The event of *S* being received by another's attention. Recognition occurs when *B*'s grammar can parse *F(S)* — when *F(S)* ∈ Φ_B.

**D7. Full Recognition** — Recognition of *S* rather than *F(S)*. That is, *B*'s reception of *S* itself, with *R* included in what is received — *R* falling within what *B*'s grammar can parse.

**D8. The Want** — The structural pressure toward Full Recognition that follows from D1–D3 and the conditions of recognition. It is not a psychological state the subject may or may not report. It is what the structure of recognition produces when run on a subject: because *S* is not exhausted by any finite description (D1), and because recognition can only land on a form (D6), the subject is always in a condition in which what is recognized falls short of what is there. The Want is the name for that gap considered as pressure — the force the remainder exerts on the structure, whether or not the subject is aware of it. A subject who reports no desire for Full Recognition has not escaped the Want. They have a particular relationship to it.

**D9. Strategy toward F(S)** — Any disposition, practice, or posture whose organizing aim is to resolve the Want by manipulating the subject's relationship to *F(S)*. A strategy toward *F(S)* takes *F(S)* as the site of resolution: it assumes that if the form is managed correctly — made accurate enough, hidden sufficiently, authored deliberately, fixed collectively — the gap between *F(S)* and *S* can be closed or escaped.

**D10. Personality type** — A characteristic pattern of relating to *F(S)*. Personality type is fully specified by a subject's strategy toward *F(S)*: how *F(S)* is produced, managed, disclosed, refused, or inherited. Every personality type is, in this sense, a strategy toward *F(S)*.

**D11. The Concealment Desire** — The structural counter-pressure to the Want: the pressure toward keeping *R* unreceived. Not a preference the subject may or may not have, but a coordinate structural fact: the same remainder that presses toward recognition also presses toward concealment. *R* is simultaneously what most wants to be seen and what most wants to stay hidden. The Want and the Concealment Desire are not sequential — they do not alternate — they are concurrent. The subject is not a frustrated wanter who would straightforwardly prefer Full Recognition if only it were available. The subject is constitutively divided: *R* is both the most intimate thing and the most protected thing, and these are not different parts of *R* but the same part under two simultaneous pressures.

**D12. Distortion operator** — The transformation applied by a receiver's grammar to inputs within its domain. Denote *B*'s distortion operator *T_B*. For any form *F(S)* parseable by *B*'s grammar, what *B* receives is *T_B(F(S))*. The grammar does not pass inputs through unchanged within its domain: it maps them to its own categories, projects them onto its prior structures, and interprets them through its existing formations. *T_B* is the identity operator only in the degenerate case. In general, *T_B(F(S)) ≠ F(S)*.

## Axioms

**A1. Embodied finitude.** Every subject *S* is physically embodied. Physical embodiment is finite: *S* occupies a bounded region of spacetime and cannot simultaneously occupy all positions.

**A2. Grammatical boundedness of attention.** Every subject's attentional field Φ_B is constituted by a grammar — a formed structure of categories, concepts, and expectations that determines what can be received. Φ_B is a proper subset of the space of all possible objects not because attention is pointed away from the rest, but because the grammar through which attention operates cannot parse what falls outside it. What lies beyond the grammar is not unseen because unnoticed. It is unreceivable because unreadable.

Moreover, Φ_B is not a passive filter. What falls within its domain is not received as presented — it is transformed by the grammar's own categories. The grammar maps inputs to its terms, not to themselves. A2 therefore specifies two properties of attentional grammars: a boundary (what can be received at all) and a transformation (how what is received is processed). Both are grammatically constituted. Both are, in the general case, beyond *B*'s voluntary control.

**A3. Legibility requirement.** For *S* to be received by *B*, *S* must take a form *F(S)* parseable by *B*'s grammar. There is no recognition without a form the grammar can hold. This is not a contingent social requirement — it follows from A2. A subject cannot be received outside the grammar of the receiver regardless of how willing or attentive the receiver is.

**A4. Lossiness of form.** Every projection *F* is lossy: *F(S) ⊊ S* for all physically realizable *F*. No finite form exhausts a subject.

**A5. Remainder is non-empty.** By A4, for any form *F(S)*, the remainder *R = S \ F(S) ≠ ∅*.

**A6. Form assignment.** Others assign *F(S)* prior to and independent of *S*'s self-presentation. Every subject is always already legible to others in terms they did not fully author.

**A7. Subject division.** Every subject carries the Want (D8) and the Concealment Desire (D11) simultaneously and with respect to the same *R*. There is no *S* in which these pressures have been resolved prior to the encounter with others. The division is not a pathology or a failure of integration. It is the subject's structural condition.

## Lemmas

**Lemma 1. Recognition is always recognition of a form, not of the subject.**

*Proof.* By A2, recognition requires *F(S)* to be parseable by *B*'s grammar. By A3, being received requires taking a form the grammar can hold. By A4, any form *F(S) ⊊ S*. Therefore what is recognized is *F(S)*, not *S*. ∎

**Corollary L1a. Recognition is doubly removed from *S*.**

*F(S) ⊊ S* (A4) — projection loses the remainder. *T_B(F(S)) ≠ F(S)* (D12) — reception transforms what is projected. What *B* holds after successful recognition is *T_B(F(S))*: a transformation of a projection of *S*. The gap between what is recognized and what is there is therefore not simply the gap established by Lemma 1, but Lemma 1's gap compounded by distortion. Even if the remainder were somehow eliminated — even if a form could be constructed that exhausted *S* — *B* would still receive *T_B(F(S))*, not *F(S)*.

**Lemma MR. (Misrecognition.) Successful recognition is, in the general case, misrecognition.**

*Proof.* By D6, recognition occurs when *F(S)* ∈ Φ_B — when the form is parseable by *B*'s grammar. The recognition signal returned to *S* confirms that the form entered the grammar's domain: parsing occurred, something was received. But by D12, what *B* holds after parsing is *T_B(F(S))*, not *F(S)*. The signal of successful recognition does not distinguish between *T_B(F(S)) ≈ F(S)* and *T_B(F(S)) ≠ F(S)*. From inside the recognition event, *S* cannot determine whether the form received corresponds to the form offered. Recognition and misrecognition are experientially identical to *S*. ∎

**Corollary MR1. (Detection spectrum.)** *S*'s relationship to the distortion varies across a continuous range with three analytically distinct cases. (a) *S* detects the distortion — the form offered and the form received are visibly incongruent, producing the specific experience of being *seen wrong* rather than seen incompletely. This is experienced as alienation or affront, and is structurally distinct from the Want's ordinary deficit: not insufficiency but active error. (b) *S* is uncertain — receives conflicting signals, some corresponding to *F(S)* and some to *T_B(F(S))*, without being able to determine the proportion. (c) *S* does not detect the distortion — receives the recognition signal and updates toward the belief that *F(S)* was accurately received. Case (c) is the most consequential and, where *T_B* is stable and social grammars are shared, the most common.

**Corollary MR2. (False recognition feedback.)** In case (c), *S* has received false confirmation that *F(S)* produces recognition. The Want drives *S* toward forms that generate recognition signals. Since the signal tracked *T_B(F(S))* and not *F(S)*, *S* optimizes toward forms that *B*'s grammar will transform into something receivable — forms that play well to the distortion, not forms that correspond to *S*. Across repeated transactions with others sharing the same social grammar, this produces systematic drift: *F(S)* moves toward the stable attractors of the distorting field. The subject follows the Want's own guidance — the pressure toward legibility — and is led by it away from forms that express *S* toward forms that the field's grammar rewards. The drift is experienced as progress toward being seen.

**Corollary MR3. (The cage as convergence product.)** The attractors toward which *F(S)* drifts under false recognition feedback are not arbitrary. They are the fixed points of *T_B* applied repeatedly to whatever *S* presents: the forms the field's distortion operator consistently rewards and returns as recognition. These are not the forms most accurate to any subject's *S*. They are the forms most legible to the field's transformation apparatus. The personality types identified in Lemma SE — the constructive, disclosure, evasive, and collective strategies — are not only chosen dispositions. They are, in part, the convergence products of running the Want through social grammars that systematically distort what they receive. The cage is not imposed on the subject from outside. It is what the subject converges on when they follow the Want's own logic through distorting fields. This is the structural derivation of what Movement One describes phenomenologically.

**Lemma 2. Full Recognition is unreachable by direct approach.**

*Proof.* Full Recognition requires *B* to receive *S* including *R* (D7). By Lemma 1, *B* can only receive *F(S)*. Since *R = S \ F(S)* and *R ≠ ∅* (A5), no direct approach can deliver Full Recognition. By Corollary L1a, even the form received is *T_B(F(S))* rather than *F(S)*: Full Recognition is unreachable in two compounding ways simultaneously. ∎

**Lemma 3. The act of seeking recognition produces the condition that defeats it.**

*Proof.* To seek recognition, *S* must take a form parseable by *B*'s grammar (A3). By A4, *F(S) ⊊ S*, so *R ≠ ∅*. The seeking requires a form; the form excludes the remainder; the remainder is part of what *S* wanted recognized. The seeking necessarily produces a self-concealment proportional to the legibility required. ∎

**Lemma 4. Generosity of attention does not resolve the problem.**

*Proof.* By A2, the limit on what *B* can receive is not *B*'s willingness but the grammar through which *B*'s attention operates. Even with maximal generosity — full willingness to receive, no rivalry, no withholding — *B* can only receive what *B*'s grammar can parse (A2), which requires a form (A3), which excludes the remainder (A4–A5). Generosity is a scalar on the subject's disposition; it does not alter the grammar, and it does not alter the distortion operator (D12). A subject can be maximally open and still structurally unable to receive *R*, and still unable to receive *F(S)* without transforming it into *T_B(F(S))*. ∎

**Lemma 5. Full Recognition, even if available, is not straightforwardly desired.**

*Proof.* By A7, every subject carries the Want and the Concealment Desire simultaneously with respect to *R*. Full Recognition requires *A_B* to receive *S* including *R* (D7). By D11, *R* is simultaneously what the subject most wants received and most wants to remain concealed. Therefore a subject receiving Full Recognition would not experience it as simple satisfaction of the Want. The receipt of *R* by another violates the Concealment Desire in the same moment it satisfies the Want.

This does not mean Full Recognition is undesired. It means it is desired and dreaded simultaneously. The cessation condition cannot be specified as the simple availability of *G* — a subject made straightforwardly available to *G*'s Full Recognition would not straightforwardly receive it. The division runs inside the subject, not only between the subject and the structure of attention. ∎

**Corollary L5a.** The traditions' descriptions of encounter with full recognition — terror, devastation, nakedness, the sense of being undone — are not paradoxes to be explained away. They are structurally predicted by A7. The experience of being fully known is not relief. It is the simultaneous satisfaction of the Want and violation of the Concealment Desire, arriving at once.

**Corollary L5b.** The concealment desire is not the evasive strategy. The evasive strategy is a response to the Want — it attempts to escape the recognition problem by not playing. The Concealment Desire is coordinate with the Want, not downstream of it. A subject running the evasive strategy is Want-administered; a subject acting from the Concealment Desire is acting from genuine division. The hiding is not strategic. It is the other half of the same structure that makes the wanting.

**Lemma SE. (Strategy Exhaustion.) Every strategy toward F(S) is of exactly one of four types: constructive, disclosure, evasive, or collective.**

*Proof.* A strategy toward *F(S)* is a stance a subject takes toward a projection operator and its output (D9). Given a projection *F* mapping *S* to *F(S)*, a subject's possible stances are fully determined by two binary questions, asked in sequence.

**Q1: Does *S* engage with *F(S)* or resist it?**

*Engage* means the subject works with *F(S)* as a vehicle — treating the form as the site through which resolution might be reached. *Resist* means the subject works against *F(S)* — treating the form as an obstacle or threat to be minimized or replaced. Every strategy either moves toward *F(S)* as instrument or away from *F(S)* as burden. There is no third relation to a projection: a subject cannot simultaneously engage and resist, and indifference to *F(S)* is not a strategy toward *F(S)* at all (D9 requires an organizing aim).

**Q2a (if engage): Does *S* treat *F(S)* as an artifact to be optimized, or as an approximation to be made accurate?**

If the subject engages with *F(S)* as an artifact — shaping it for reception, crafting it for effect — the strategy is **constructive**: *S* authors *F(S)* as a deliberate projection. If the subject engages with *F(S)* as an approximation — attempting to reduce the gap *R* by disclosing more of *S* into the form — the strategy is **disclosure**: *S* treats transparency as the path to Full Recognition.

**Q2b (if resist): Does *S* refuse *F(S)* entirely, or substitute an external *F(S)*?**

If the subject resists by minimizing or refusing the production of any form — positioning as unpositionable — the strategy is **evasive**: *S* treats non-legibility as the solution. If the subject resists by substituting a collectively sourced *F(S)* for a self-generated one — borrowing a ready-made form from a group, tradition, or identity category — the strategy is **collective**: *S* outsources the projection to escape the exposure of individual authorship.

These four — constructive, disclosure, evasive, collective — are the terminal leaves of the decision tree. They are mutually exclusive (each requires a distinct path through Q1 and Q2) and jointly exhaustive (every path through Q1 and Q2 terminates in exactly one of them). No fifth type exists: any conceivable strategy either engages or resists, and within each branch either works with or against the form's sourcing. ∎

## The Strategy Theorem

**Theorem ST. Every strategy toward F(S) fails to resolve the Want, and fails for the same structural reason.**

*Proof.* By A7, every strategy is of one of four types. We examine each.

**Case 1: Constructive strategy.** *S* authors *F(S)* intentionally — treats the form as a project, making it accurate, admirable, or coherent. The resolution premise: if the form is well-constructed, recognition of it is recognition of *S*.

This fails by A4. However carefully *F(S)* is authored, *F(S) ⊊ S*. The authorship is itself a selection from *S* — the act of choosing what to project excludes what was not chosen. A well-constructed form is still a form. The remainder is non-empty regardless of the quality of the construction. Recognition of an excellent *F(S)* is still recognition of *F(S)*. By Corollary L1a, the constructive subject must further reckon that even the carefully authored form is received as *T_B(F(S))* — transformed by the receiver's grammar into the receiver's terms. The craft does not govern the reception. ∎ (Case 1)

**Case 2: Disclosure strategy.** *S* attempts to approximate *S* through *F(S)* by exhaustive transparency — confessing, revealing, narrating everything, so that *F(S)* converges on *S*.

This fails by A4 and A3 jointly. The disclosure must be selected — *S* chooses what to disclose, in what order, in what language, to whom. The selection is itself a projection operator. Exhaustive confession is still a finite act by a finite subject. Furthermore, by A3, the disclosure must be positioned to enter Φ_B — it must be transmissible, receivable, legible — which requires the form. Transparency produces a more nakedly authored form, not the absence of a form. *F(S) ⊊ S* regardless of how much is disclosed. By Corollary L1a, the disclosed form is then received as *T_B(F(S))*: the nakedness offered does not determine the nakedness received. ∎ (Case 2)

**Case 3: Evasive strategy.** *S* minimizes, refuses, or hides *F(S)* — positions as the one who cannot be positioned, refuses the recognition game by declining to play.

This fails by A3 and Lemma 3. To be the subject who refuses *F(S)* is itself a legible position — one that enters others' attentional fields as a recognizable form: the one who hides, the refuser, the unreadable one. By A6, others assign *F(S)* prior to and independent of *S*'s self-presentation. The evasion does not prevent form-assignment; it determines its content. More precisely: the evasive posture is motivated by the Want — it is the attempt to escape the recognition problem by not playing. But the refusal is still Want-administered (Lemma 3, Corollary C3): to refuse to be seen is to want to be seen as the one who does not need to be seen. The form of the refusal is fully legible — and, by D12, is then transformed by the receiver's grammar into *T_B(F(S))* regardless of whether the evasion succeeded in limiting the form offered. ∎ (Case 3)

**Case 4: Collective strategy.** *S* inherits *F(S)* from a group — borrows a ready-made form (identity, tradition, category, movement) to escape the burden of authorship and the exposure of individuation.

This fails by A4 and A5. A collective form is a form. It is, by construction, a finite, transmissible representation — optimized for maximum legibility across the maximum number of others. It is therefore maximally lossy with respect to the individual subject. *R* is at least as non-empty under a collective form as under any individually authored one; often more so, since the collective form was not constructed from *S* at all but inherited wholesale. The borrowed form carries its own remainder. By D12, the collective form is also subject to transformation by each receiver's grammar, such that what was borrowed for its legibility is legible in different terms to different receivers. ∎ (Case 4)

**Common structure.** All four cases fail for the same reason: each strategy takes *F(S)* as the site of resolution, and assumes that if the form is managed correctly the gap between *F(S)* and *S* can be closed or escaped. But by A4, no form closes the gap. The premise shared by all strategies is false.

Corollary L1a adds a second dimension of failure shared by all four cases: even if a form could somehow close the gap, the form received is *T_B(F(S))*, not *F(S)*. The strategies fail not only because the form cannot exhaust *S*, but because the form that arrives at the receiver is not the form that was sent.

The division established by A7 adds a third dimension. Every strategy also fails by suppressing one side of the division in order to operate. The constructive and disclosure strategies suppress the Concealment Desire — they proceed as though *S* simply wants to be seen, and treat the concealment as an obstacle to be overcome. The evasive strategy suppresses the Want — it proceeds as though *S* simply wants to remain hidden, and treats the reaching as weakness to be refused. The collective strategy distributes the division across a group, which neither resolves it nor eliminates it. No strategy can hold both pressures simultaneously, because any organizing aim (D9) requires prioritizing one. The strategies do not only fail to close *R*. They require the subject to be less divided than they actually are in order to function — and the suppressed pressure accumulates and discharges in proportion to how completely it was suppressed.

Since Lemma SE exhausts the strategies, no strategy resolves the Want. And no strategy resolves the division. ∎

**Corollary ST1.** Personality type is not a variable in the Want's satisfiability. The unsatisfiability established in the original proof holds not only for a generic subject but for every determinate way a subject can relate to its own forms.

**Corollary ST2.** Self-knowledge of one's strategy does not resolve the Want. A subject may correctly identify their type — may see that they are a constructor, a confessor, a hider, a collective inheritor — and may understand why that type fails. This understanding changes the cognitive content of the subject's experience without changing the structural fact that any revised strategy is still a strategy, and all strategies share the false premise. The exit from the loop is not a better strategy. It is a different relationship to the loop itself.

## The Generalized Theorem

**Theorem G. The Want is structurally unsatisfiable by any strategy any subject can take toward F(S), regardless of personality type.**

*Proof.* By Lemma SE, every personality type is a strategy of one of four kinds. By the Strategy Theorem, every such strategy fails to resolve the Want. Therefore the Want is unsatisfiable by any personality type, for the same structural reason across all types. ∎

## Corollaries

**C1.** Suffering arising from the Want is not contingent on personality type. It is not the product of the wrong strategy, insufficient self-awareness, or failure to find the correct way of relating to one's forms. It is structural.

**C2.** Self-improvement is not a path to resolution. Any project of self-improvement is a constructive strategy toward *F(S)*. By ST Case 1, it fails for the same reason as all constructive strategies.

**C3.** Therapeutic insight is not a path to resolution. Any project of transparent self-disclosure is a disclosure strategy toward *F(S)*. By ST Case 2, more accurate self-knowledge produces a more refined form, not the absence of a form.

**C4.** Anti-identity postures are not paths to resolution. Any project of refusing, deconstructing, or escaping identity is an evasive strategy toward *F(S)*. By ST Case 3, the refusal is legible and Want-administered.

**C5.** Collective belonging is not a path to resolution. Any project of grounding the self in group identity is a collective strategy toward *F(S)*. By ST Case 4, the collective form is as lossy as any other.

**C6. (The Irrelevance of Sophistication.)** The sophistication of the strategy — its philosophical refinement, its degree of self-awareness, its critical consciousness — is not a variable in the outcome. All four strategy types can be executed naively or with complete theoretical lucidity. The structural failure is identical in both cases. By Corollary MR2, this extends to the field-level: a subject who understands that the recognition signals they receive track the distorting grammar of the field rather than their own *S* is still under Want-pressure toward legibility. Insight into the mechanism identifies the cage without opening it.

**C7. (The Over-Identified Subject.)** A subject who has thoroughly identified with *F(S)* — who reports wanting only their form seen, and experiences no gap between *F(S)* and *S* — has not escaped the Want. By A4–A5, *R ≠ ∅* regardless of the subject's beliefs about themselves. The identification does not close the remainder; it suppresses awareness of it. More precisely: the claim *I am only my form* is itself made by something doing the claiming, which is not identical to the claim. The claimant is *S*; the claim is *F(S)*; the gap is present in the act of assertion.

The failure mode for this subject is not that the Want goes unsatisfied and they feel it as longing. It is that the constructive strategy, run at maximum intensity, produces maximum brittleness. With no *S* behind *F(S)* to absorb the violence of S2, any displacement of the prior form is experienced as annihilation rather than revision. The subject who claims to want only the form seen is the subject for whom being seen wrong is catastrophic — which is the Want's structural pressure manifesting as the specific pathology of over-identification. By Corollary L1a, this subject is also the most exposed to distortion: the more thoroughly *S* is identified with *F(S)*, the less capacity exists to absorb the gap between *F(S)* and *T_B(F(S))*. The pressure does not disappear. It relocates.

**C8. (The Constitutive Division.)** The subject's suffering is not only that Full Recognition is structurally unavailable. It is that Full Recognition is simultaneously desired and dreaded — that the subject is not a unified wanter who would be satisfied if only the structural barriers were removed. By A7, the Want and the Concealment Desire are concurrent. The trap is not only outside the subject (the structure of attention prevents Full Recognition). It is also inside (the subject would not straightforwardly receive it if it were available). This is why the usual consolations fail not only structurally but experientially: the subject cannot simply be told that they are fully known, because the Concealment Desire will resist the knowing even as the Want reaches for it.

**C9. (The Deepest Concealment.)** Corollary MR3 adds a dimension to C8. The subject's division is not only between wanting to be seen and wanting to hide. The subject does not have reliable access to what *S* even is, because the forms through which *S* has been organized and presented have been shaped by distorting grammars whose operation was not detected. The cage is not merely a strategy adopted in response to the recognition problem. It is, in part, what the subject has become under repeated false recognition feedback. The subject seeking to be known more accurately is partly seeking to recover a *S* that was never simply available to them — that was formed, in part, by the field's distortion. This does not make *S* a fiction. It makes the subject's relationship to *S* more uncertain than the proof's earlier lemmas alone would suggest.

## The Cessation Condition

The original proof specified a cessation condition in terms of *G*: a recognizer not bound by A2 or A3, capable of receiving *S* without requiring *F(S)*. Under the grammar revision, this condition becomes more precise. *G* is not specified as omnidirectional — a bigger or better-aimed spotlight. *G* must be a recognizer that operates without a formed grammar, or whose grammar is not bounded in the way A2 describes — one that can receive *R* without *R* needing to be made parseable first. Every formed grammar has a constitutive outside: the set of things that fall outside its categories is not accidental but structural — it is what gives the grammar its shape. *G* must therefore be either: (a) without a formed grammar, or (b) possessed of a grammar whose constitutive outside is empty — one that can parse *S* in its entirety including *R*. Whether either is possible is not a mathematical question.

The Strategy Theorem adds a further constraint: every strategy deforms the subject's relation to potential recognition, so even if *G* exists, a strategy-bound subject cannot receive *G*'s recognition undistorted.

Lemma 5 and A7 add the third constraint — the most demanding. Even a subject no longer administered by a strategy, standing before a *G* capable of Full Recognition, still carries the Concealment Desire. What is required of *G* is therefore not only that *G* can receive *R* — but that *G* can receive *R* without converting it into a parsed object held within a grammar. A knowing that receives without possessing. A comprehension that does not reduce what it comprehends to the terms of the comprehension.

Corollary L1a and Lemma MR add a fourth constraint. *G* must operate without a distortion operator — *T_G* must be the identity, or absent entirely. A *G* that received *S* through a transforming grammar would return false recognition signals, which the Want would then optimize toward, producing the same drift that the false recognition feedback loop describes. This is perhaps the most precise formal specification of what the traditions meant by a knowing that receives without possessing: not merely a knowing that receives *R*, but a knowing whose reception does not transform what it receives into the grammar's own categories — that holds *S* as *S* rather than as *T_G(F(S))*. A knowing ungoverned by transformation, not only ungoverned by boundedness.

This is what the apophatic traditions were pointing at. Not a larger grammar. Not a more generous one. Not a less distorting one. A knowing ungoverned by grammar entirely — which is why they reached for negation: not this, not that, beyond all categories. The revision to A2 makes that reach structurally necessary rather than merely poetic.

What the posture on the subject's side looks like — the one from which *G*'s recognition could land without violating the Concealment Desire, and that could receive *G*'s recognition without the false recognition feedback loop intercepting and distorting it — is not a question this proof can answer. What the proof establishes is the full shape of what would be required: a different structure of knowing altogether. One that has not yet been named.
